# Hindi → English: the plan

American English taught to a Hindi speaker, explanations written in natural Hindi (Devanagari, the
everyday Hindi a good teacher writes: क्रिया, संज्ञा, सवाल, ग़लती, with the English grammar names Indian
schools use, such as {{present continuous}}, where they help). No readings (`"reading": ""`): the learner
reads the Latin alphabet; pronunciation comes in tips.

Every lesson is a capability, opens with its goal sentence, then meaning, form, the mistakes a Hindi
speaker makes, and practice ending with the learner's own sentence. The comparisons are always with Hindi.

## What is hard for a Hindi speaker (and what this course does about it)

- **Word order**: Hindi puts the verb last (मैं दिल्ली से हूँ); English puts it right after the subject
  (*I am from Delhi*). From lesson 1, and again in questions (*Where do you live?*, not *Where you live?*)
  and in reported questions (*Tell me where the station is*).
- **Prepositions before the noun** (*in the room*, not *the room in*), and one Hindi से for *from, with,
  since, by, than* (lessons 11, 18, 35, 36).
- **a / an / the**: Hindi has none (एक is "one"). Lessons 4 and 7, and in every lesson's mistakes.
- **he / she**: वह is both; English must choose, and *his / her* follow the **owner**, not the thing
  owned (उसकी किताब = *his book* or *her book*). Lessons 2 and 8.
- **do / does / did** in questions and negatives (lessons 15, 23), the third-person **-s** (14).
- **"To me" sentences turn around**: मुझे चाहिए → *I need*, मुझे पसंद है → *I like*, मुझे भूख लगी है →
  *I'm hungry*, मुझे पता है → *I know* (lessons 5, 16, 32).
- **Tenses Indian English uses differently**: *I have seen him yesterday* (→ *I saw*), *I had gone to
  Agra last week* (→ *I went*), *I am living here since two years* (→ *I have lived here for two years*),
  *I am knowing* (→ *I know*), *If it will rain* (→ *If it rains*), *isn't it?* after everything.
- **Politeness** lives in words (*please, could you, would you mind*), not in आप / तुम (lessons 3, 30).
- **Pronunciation**: व is both *v* and *w* (*very* / *wary*), थ is not *th*, the *-ed* endings, no vowel
  before *s* + consonant (*school*, not *iskool*). As tips, where the words come up.
- **American words** where Indian English uses British ones (*apartment, cell phone, check, line,
  vacation, Mom*): as tips.

## The spiral: where each big structure comes back

- **be** (1) → *he / she* (2) → questions (3) → *was / were* (19) → *going to* (27) → the passive (49).
- **Word order** (1) → questions with *be* (3) → *do* questions (15) → *did* questions (23) → reported
  questions (44) → follow-up questions (50).
- **a / an / the** (4, 7) → plurals (6) → *there is* (10) → uncountables (37) → in every lesson's mistakes.
- **he / she, his / her** (2, 8) → *-s* (14) → *me, him, her* (38) → *he said* (44).
- **"To me" turned round**: *I need* (5) → *I have* (9) → *I like* (16) → *I want to* (20) → *I should*
  (31) → *I'm hungry* (32) → *I think* (47).
- **do** (15) → *did* (23) → *Do you have…?* (9 → 15) → tags *don't you? didn't you?* (45).
- **The present**: simple (14) → continuous (17) → for plans (27) → *since* with the perfect, not the
  continuous (35).
- **The past**: *was* (19) → *-ed* (21) → irregular (22) → *did* (23) → a weekend story (24) → *used to*
  (25) → *was doing* (26) → *I saw* vs *I've seen* (34) → a story with *had* (46) → *she said she was*
  (44).
- **The future**: *going to* (27) → *will* (28) → *if it rains, … will* (41) → *would* (42).
- **Modals and politeness**: *can* (29) → *could you* (30) → *should* (31) → *would* (42).
- **Prepositions**: place (11) → time (18) → *for / since* (35) → *than* (36) → verbs that take them (38,
  40).

From lesson 4 on, every lesson has at least one practice question marked `from` an earlier lesson.
