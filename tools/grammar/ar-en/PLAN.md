# ar-en: English for Arabic speakers (قواعد اللغة الإنجليزية)

**Who it is for**: an Arabic speaker (any dialect; reads Modern Standard Arabic) who knows no English
grammar and wants to hold everyday conversations in American English, about A2 touching B1.
**Explanations**: clear, simple Modern Standard Arabic, as a good teacher writes it; vowel marks only where
they help (a word that could be misread). **Examples**: natural American English (movie, apartment,
vacation, soccer, the check, color, favorite), no readings (`"reading": ""`).

## What is hard for an Arabic speaker, and where the course spends its time

1. **"be" must be said.** Arabic says أنا طالب with no verb; English needs *I am a student*. The opposite
   mistake comes right after: putting *am/is* before every verb (*I am work here*). Lessons 1–5, 7, 10;
   back as *be + -ing* (18), *was/were* (22), *was + -ing* (27), *going to* (28), the passive (49).
2. **a/an and the.** Arabic has الـ but no word for "a" (its tanween isn't written as a word), so learners
   drop *a* (*He is doctor*). And Arabic uses الـ for things in general (أحب القهوة، الحياة جميلة) where
   English uses no article (*I like coffee*, *Life is beautiful*). a/an with jobs (2), ordering (6), a vs
   the (11), no *the* for things in general (16), *go home / to work / to school* (12), uncountables (32),
   *the* + superlative (34).
3. **do in questions and negatives.** Arabic asks with هل or just the voice (أين تسكن؟); English needs *Where
   do you live?* and *I don't…*. Lessons 14, 15, 17; *did* (25); *What are you doing?* vs *What do you do?*
   (29); *I don't think…* (44); questions without *do* when *who* is the subject (50).
4. **Present simple vs continuous.** The Arabic مضارع covers both *I work* and *I'm working*. 12–17 vs 18;
   back for future arrangements (29) and stories (27).
5. **Past and present perfect.** Arabic has one past (فعلتُ) and says منذ with the present (أسكن هنا منذ خمس
   سنوات → *I've lived here for five years*). Past: 22–27; present perfect: 37–40; *used to* for كنتُ أفعل
   (41).
6. **Word order.** The adjective before the noun and never plural (*red cars*: 7); *'s* for the iḍāfa (9);
   *always* before the verb (17); question order; *tell me where the station is* (47).
7. **it for things.** Arabic calls a car هي and a book هو; English calls both *it*. 5, 7 (*It's hot*), 16 (*I
   love it*).
8. **Numbers.** Arabic says عشرون كتابًا (singular after 11); English says *twenty books*. 8, 32.
9. **No *to* after can, must, should** (from أستطيع أن، يجب أن): 19, 35, 36.
10. ***than*, not *from*** (أطول من): 33.
11. **No "it" left in a relative clause** (الكتاب الذي قرأتُه → *the book that I read*, not *…read it*): 43.
12. ***if* + present for the future** (Arabic says إذا + past): 45, 46.
13. **Reading and pronunciation notes** (tips): left to right and capital letters (1), *th* = ث / ذ (5),
    *p* vs *b* (6), short vowels (7), *v* vs *f* (8), the *-s* ending (13), *sp, st* without a vowel before
    them (19), the *-ed* ending (23).

## The spiral: where each big structure comes back

| structure | first | back for a harder job |
|---|---|---|
| *be* (am/is/are) | 1–5 | 7 (+ adjective), 10 (there is), 18 (+ -ing), 22 (was/were), 27 (was -ing), 28 (going to), 49 (passive) |
| questions | 3 (yes/no with be), 4 (wh- + be) | 15 (do), 17 (How often), 25 (did), 29 (What are you doing?), 37 (Have you ever?), 38 (How long?), 47 (indirect), 50 (interview) |
| *do / does / did* | 14, 15 | 16, 17, 25, 44 (I don't think), 50 |
| *a / an / the* | 2, 6 | 11, 12 (go home), 16 (things in general), 32 (uncountables), 34 (the best) |
| present simple | 12–17 | 18 (vs continuous), 26, 45 (if + present) |
| present continuous | 18 | 27 (past continuous), 29 (future arrangements) |
| past simple | 22–25 | 26 (why), 27 (story), 40 (vs present perfect), 41 (used to), 46–47 (if I had, she said she was) |
| future | 28–30 | 31 (invitations), 45 (if… will) |
| *can* | 6 (Can I have…?), 19 | 21 (could), 35–36 (modals without to) |
| *have* | 8 | 36 (have to), 37–39 (have + participle) |
| comparing | 33 | 34, 44 (opinions: better, worse) |
| the past participle | 37 | 38–40, 49 (passive) |

Every lesson from the 4th on has practice with `from` that brings back an earlier lesson's grammar.
