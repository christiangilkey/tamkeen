# English → German: the plan

German explained in English, for someone who has never studied grammar. Standard German as it is
spoken in Germany (the variety of the German basics course), with *du* among friends and *Sie* with
strangers from the first lesson. 48 lessons in 9 units: 20 A1, 19 A2, 9 B1.

## What makes German German (and so decides the order)

- **The verb is the second thing in a statement.** It is taught in the very first sentence and comes
  back in every unit: after a question word, after a time word (*Morgen fahre ich…*), and against the
  two places where the verb is *not* second (yes/no questions: first; after *weil, dass, wenn*: last).
- **The verb bracket.** With a modal (*Ich möchte heute Kaffee trinken*), a separable verb (*Ich stehe
  um sieben auf*) and the Perfekt (*Ich habe Pizza gegessen*), one part of the verb stays second and the
  other goes to the very end. The bracket is introduced with modals (the easiest and most useful), then
  reused for separable verbs and the past, so the third time it already feels normal.
- **Gender and cases, introduced by what they let you do.** *der/die/das* to name things; the
  accusative the moment you want or have something (*Ich möchte einen Kaffee*: only the masculine
  changes, so it costs little); the dative when you travel *mit dem Bus*, give something *meiner
  Mutter*, say *Das gefällt mir*, and say where things are (*auf dem Tisch*); the genitive only to
  recognise (B1). Adjective endings come last and a little at a time (A2, then B1).
- **The past in speech is the Perfekt**, with *haben* or *sein*; *war, hatte* and the modals' past
  (*konnte, musste*) are taught as the exception everyone uses; the Präteritum of other verbs is for
  reading and stories (B1).
- **Konjunktiv II** first as polite chunks (*ich hätte gern* in A1, *könnten Sie* in A2), then for wishes,
  advice and imagining (B1).

## The spiral (where each big structure comes back)

- **Verb second**: 1, 2 (after a question word) → 16 (after a time word) → 21 (with the Perfekt) → 31
  (*denn* keeps it, *weil* breaks it) → 33 (a whole clause in first place) → 42 (*deshalb, trotzdem*).
- **The verb bracket**: 13–15 (modals) → 17 (separable verbs) → 21–24 (Perfekt) → 35 (*werden*) →
  45 (*würde*) → 48 (passive).
- **Verb last**: 31 (*weil*) → 32 (*dass*) → 33 (*wenn*) → 34 (*ob*, *wo*) → 41 (relative clauses) →
  42 (*obwohl*) → 44 (*als, nachdem*) → 47 (reporting).
- **Questions**: 2 (question words) → 4 (yes/no) → 7 (*Was kostet…?*) → 19 (asking the way) → 34
  (indirect questions) → 47 (reporting a question).
- **Accusative**: 9 (have) → 10 (order) → 11 (*ihn, sie*) → 12 (*meinen*) → 19 (*es gibt*) → 30
  (*wohin?*) → 39 (adjective endings).
- **Dative**: 26 (*mit, zu*) → 27 (giving) → 28 (*gefallen*) → 29 (*wo?*) → 40 (adjective endings) → 41
  (*mit dem*, relative).
- **The past**: 21–24 (Perfekt) → 25 (*war, hatte*) → 44 (story, Präteritum) → 47 (what someone said)
  → 48 (*wurde gebaut*).
- **Liking and wanting**: 10 (*möchte*) → 11 (*gern, mögen*) → 13 (*möchten* + verb) → 28 (*gefallen*)
  → 37 (*lieber, am liebsten*) → 38 (*hätte gern, würde gern*) → 45 (dreams).
- **Politeness**: 1 (*du/Sie*) → 10 (*hätte gern*) → 19 (*Sie* command) → 20 (*bitte, mal*) → 34
  (indirect questions) → 38 (Konjunktiv II) → 46 (advice).

Every lesson from the fourth on has at least one practice question with `from` (an earlier lesson's
grammar in the new situation).
