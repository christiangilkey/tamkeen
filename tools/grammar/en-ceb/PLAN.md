# English → Bisaya (Cebuano): the plan

Bisaya as spoken in Cebu, in the spelling of the Bisaya basics course (`tools/course/ceb/words.json`):
`og` for "a/some" before an object and `ug` for "and"; `gyud`, `dinhi` (`diri` in speech), `ganahan`,
`selpon`, `banyo`. Completed verbs are written with **mi-** (as the basics course does), and the lessons
say that Cebu speech says **ni-** just as often (`Nikaon ka na?` is in the dialogues). The ongoing **ga-**
of Cebu speech is shown for recognition; the examples write **nag-**. "Also" is `sad` in conversation
(`usab` in writing, `pud` too).

## How a Bisaya sentence works (what the course builds, in this order)

1. **The describing word comes first, and there is no "am/is/are"**: `Maestra ko.` "I'm a teacher."
2. **The little words** (`ko, ka, mi, ta, mo`, then `na, pa, ba, man, lang, gyud…`) sit right after the
   first word of the sentence, whatever that word is (`Dili ko…`, `Asa ka…`, `Alas sais ko mobangon`).
3. **Markers** say what each noun does: `ang/si` (what the sentence is about), `sa/ni` (the doer of a
   gi- verb, "of"), `sa/kang` (to, at, for), `og` (a/some). They are a spiral: met one job at a time.
4. **Three sets of pronouns** (`ako/ko`, `nako/akong`, `kanako`), one per marker.
5. **Verbs say whether the action is done or not yet**, not when: `mi-` done, `mo-` not yet (future,
   habits, after `gusto`, `dili`, `wala`), `nag-` going on (now or then).
6. **Focus**: when the thing acted on is particular ("the", "this", "my", a person), the verb points at it
   (`gi-`, `-on`, `-a`; `-an`, `-i`; `i-`), and the doer goes into the `nako` set. Actor focus first
   (units 2–3), object focus after (unit 4), then used everywhere.

## The spiral (where each big structure comes back)

- **No "to be", describing word first**: 1 → 2 (people and things) → 4 (`dili`) → 36 (feelings) → 37 (compare).
- **The little words after the first word**: 1 (`Taga-Cebu ko`) → 3 (`ba`) → 4 (after `dili`) → 14 (after a time)
  → 18 (after a question word) → 31 (all the little words) → 34 (`Nakaadto ka na?`).
- **Markers**: `si` 1 → `ang/si` 2 → `sa` (place) 8 → `og` 9 → `ni/sa` (doer) 21–22 → `kang` 25 → `nga` 39, 42.
- **Pronoun sets**: `ako/ko` 1 → the whole set 2 → `akong/nako` 6 → `nako` as the doer 21 → `kanako` 25 → `tika` 24.
- **Saying no**: `dili` 4 → `wala` (none) 7 → `wala` (not here) 8 → `dili` + `mo-` 13 → `wala` + `mo-` 17 → `ayaw` 19 → `wala pa ko ka-` 34.
- **Done / going on / not yet**: `mo-` 12–14 → `nag-` 15 → `mi-` 16–17 → `gi-` 21 → `-on/-a` 23 → `-an/-i` 24 → `i-` 25
  → `mag-` 29 → `mang-` 30 → all of them in a story 32 → `na-` 33 → `naka-` 34 → `kung` + `mo-` 40 → `kung … unta` 47.
- **Questions**: 3 → `unsa ni` 5 → `naa bay` 7 → `asa` 8 → `pila` 10 → with verbs 18 → `unsaon pag-` 20 → `kinsa ang` 22
  → `Nakaadto ka na?` 34 → `kanus-a`, `pila na ka tuig` 35 → `kinsa ang…` (who did it) 42.
- **`gusto`**: + a thing 9 → + a verb 12 → `ganahan` vs `gusto` 27 → `unta` 46.
- **The linker `nga`**: describing 11 → `ang babaye nga nagkanta` 39 → `ang libro nga akong gibasa` 42 → `miingon siya nga` 43.
- **`na` and `pa`**: 16–17 → 31 → 34.
- **Commands**: 19 → directions 20 → `-a` 23 → `-i` 24 → `i-` 25 → `pa-` 45.
