# English → Hindi: the plan

Hindi taught to an English speaker, explanations in English. Everyday Hindi of India, in Devanagari, as
the basics course writes it (nukta spellings: ज़्यादा, ख़ुश, फ़ोन; -ए forms: गए, लिए, चाहिए, दीजिए).
Every example has its reading (`r`) in the basics course's romanisation (ā ī ū, ṭ ḍ ṛ ṇ, ṅ, a tilde for
a nasal vowel: mẽ, hū̃, haĩ; ch, chh, sh, kh for ख and ख़, gh for घ and ग़, z, f, q; the silent short a
left out: karnā, samay). Every Hindi piece in an explanation has its reading too ({{मैं|maĩ}}).

Grammar is a tool for doing things: each lesson is a capability, opens with its goal sentence, then the
meaning, the form, the common mistakes, and practice that ends with the learner's own sentence.
Examples speak to and about men and women: where a form depends on who speaks, the lesson shows both
(मैं जाता हूँ said by a man, मैं जाती हूँ by a woman).

## What makes Hindi Hindi (and what this course does about it)

- **The verb comes last** (lesson 1 on). Everything else (who, when, where, what) comes before it.
- **Gender** (masculine, feminine) changes adjectives, *of* (का/की/के) and verbs. Lesson 6, then every
  verb lesson: -ता/-ती, रहा/रही, था/थी, गया/गई, -गा/-गी.
- **Respect**: आप / तुम / तू from lesson 2, and again with every verb form (commands, the present, the
  future, the past with ने, requests).
- **Postpositions** come after the noun (lesson 8) and change the noun before them (the oblique form,
  lesson 9); का/की/के agrees with what is owned (lesson 10).
- **"To me" sentences** (मुझे चाहिए, मुझे पसंद है, मुझे जाना है, मुझे लगता है, मुझे बुख़ार है): the person
  is not the subject. A spiral: lessons 4, 16, 23, 33, 34.
- **ने in the past**, the big one, at A2 (lessons 27–30), after the easy past (26), then everywhere.

## The spiral: where each big structure comes back

- **है / हूँ / हैं** (1) → respect forms (2) → questions and नहीं (3) → this/that (5) → plurals (7) →
  था (19) → होगा "will be / must be" (21, 48) → होता "would be" (49).
- **Respect** (2) → family, elders in the plural (10) → commands (12) → the present (14) → the future
  (21) → requests and suggestions (24) → आपने in the past (27).
- **Gender** (6) → plurals (7) → का/की (10) → -ता/-ती (14) → रहा/रही (17) → था/थी (19) → चाहता/चाहती
  (20) → -गा/-गी (21) → गया/गई (26) → the past with ने agreeing with the *thing* (27).
- **Postpositions** (8) → the oblique (9) → का (10) → के पास (11) → मुझे (16) → से "since" (34) →
  -ने से पहले / के बाद / के लिए (36) → से in comparisons (37).
- **"To me" sentences**: चाहिए (4) → पसंद (16) → मुझे जाना है / चाहिए / पड़ेगा (23) → लगना (33) → दर्द,
  बुख़ार (34) → मुझे लगता है कि (48).
- **Questions**: क्या (3) → कहाँ (8) → कितना (13) → कब, क्यों (15) → in the past (29) → passed on (41).
- **No**: नहीं (3) → मत (12) → है left out (15) → न in the past and in suggestions (29, 24) → न in
  conditions (43).
- **The past**: था (19) → गया (26) → ने (27, 28) → questions (29) → have done (30) → used to (31) →
  was doing (32) → compound verbs (40) → a story (46) → would have (49) → the passive (50).
- **The subjunctive**: suggestions (24) → plans (25) → wanting others to (42) → if (43) → perhaps (48).
- **The future**: 21 → plans (25) → passing on a promise (41) → if (43).
- **The -ना form**: the dictionary form (14) → मुझे नाचना पसंद है (16) → चाहना (20) → मुझे जाना है (23) →
  मिलने आना (25) → -ने से पहले (36) → -ने लगना (46).

From lesson 4 on, every lesson has at least one practice question marked `from` an earlier lesson.
