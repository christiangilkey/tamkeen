# English → French: the plan

French (France), explained in English for someone who knows no grammar. 48 lessons in 8 units: 20 A1,
19 A2, 9 B1. Every lesson is a capability (its title), opens with the sentence the learner can say by the
end, then meaning, form, mistakes, practice ending in "your turn".

## Why this order

- **Talk from the first minute.** *Je suis*, *je m'appelle*, *je voudrais* are learned as whole phrases
  first; *être*, *avoir* and the conditional are explained properly later (and *je voudrais* comes back
  as a true conditional in unit 7).
- **Gender and articles early**, because no French noun can be said without them, and **the partitive**
  (*du, de la*) by the time the learner orders food. *De* after a negative (*pas de café*) comes back each
  time a negative does.
- **Avoir where English says "be"** (*j'ai 30 ans, j'ai faim*) in the first unit: the commonest early
  mistake.
- **Present of -er verbs** before the irregulars, since nine verbs in ten are -er; then the irregulars a
  learner needs to get about: *aller, venir, faire, prendre, vouloir, pouvoir, devoir*.
- **Aller + infinitive** (the near future) before the past: it is easy (one verb they know) and gives the
  learner plans to talk about at once.
- **The past as a spiral**: passé composé with *avoir*, irregular participles, with *être*, questions about
  a trip, then the imparfait, then both together to tell a story; reflexives come back in the past;
  reported speech and the pluperfect at B1.
- **Pronouns after the past**: *le/la/les*, *lui/leur*, *y/en* need the learner to know where they go in
  the passé composé (*je l'ai vu*).
- **Depuis + present** gets its own lesson: English speakers say *j'habite ici depuis…* wrongly in the past.
- **The conditional, then si-clauses, then the subjunctive** at B1, each through what it lets you do
  (be polite, imagine, say what must happen, what you want someone to do).
- **Spoken French** last (no *ne*, *on*, *t'as*, *y a*), so the learner can follow real conversation;
  earlier lessons point it out in tips.

## The spiral: where each structure comes back (`from`)

- **être**: 1 (*je suis*) → 4 (all persons) → 6 (negative) → 9 (with adjectives) → 25 (as the past's
  helper) → 27 (*c'était*) → 30.
- **avoir**: 5 → 6 (*pas de*) → 21 (*avoir besoin de, avoir mal à*) → 23 (as the past's helper) → 26.
- **tu / vous**: 2 → 4 → 7 (questions) → 20 (invitations) → 22 (two imperatives) → 40.
- **je voudrais**: 3 (a phrase) → 15 (ordering) → 20 → 40 (the conditional explained) → 45 (*je voudrais que*).
- **articles and gender**: 3 → 8 → 9 → 10 → 14 (*le* for things in general) → 15 (partitive) → 33 (*en*
  replaces them).
- **de after a negative**: 6 → 15 → 17 → 33.
- **questions**: 7 → 13 → 16 → 26 (in the past) → 40 (inversion with *pourriez-vous*) → 46 (*demander si*)
  → 48 (inversion in formal French).
- **contractions au/du**: 11 → 16 → 17 → 22 (directions).
- **aller**: 16 → 19 (+ infinitive) → 25 (*je suis allé*) → 37 (with *venir de*, *être en train de*) → 38.
- **present of -er verbs**: 13 → 14 → 18 → 22 (imperative) → 27 (imparfait stem) → 38 (future stem).
- **passé composé**: 23 → 24 → 25 → 26 → 28 → 30 → 31 (*je l'ai vu*) → 36 (*il y a*) → 46 → 47.
- **imparfait**: 27 → 28 → 41 (*si j'avais*) → 46 → 47.
- **reflexive verbs**: 1 (*je m'appelle*, a phrase) and 22 (*asseyez-vous*, a phrase) → 29 → 30 → 48.
- **object pronouns**: 20 (*tu peux m'aider ?*, a phrase) → 31 → 32 → 33 → 40 → 46.
- **the future**: 19 (*aller* + inf.) → 38 → 39 → 46 (future → conditional).
- **conditional**: 3 → 40 → 41 → 46.
- **subjunctive**: 21 (*il faut* + infinitive) → 43 (to recognise) → 44 → 45.

## Conventions

- French as said in France: *soixante-dix, quatre-vingts*; *le petit-déjeuner, le déjeuner, le dîner*;
  *un portable*; *faire les courses*. Standard written French in the examples (*ne* kept) except where a
  scene is casual; lesson 48 is about what changes in speech.
- Typography as the French basics course: a no-break space (U+00A0) before `?` and `!` (and `:` `;`);
  straight apostrophes (*j'ai*).
- Words come mostly from the French basics course's 1000 words.
- No audio files: examples are read by the learner's card voice.
