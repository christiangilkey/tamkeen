# English → Catalan: the plan

Catalan taught to an English speaker, explanations in English. Central Catalan (Barcelona), in the
current spelling (IEC 2017: *soc*, *dona* "gives", *venen*, *ves*; only the fifteen diacritic accents
*bé, déu, és, mà, més, món, pèl, què, sé, sí, sòl, són, té, ús, vós* are kept), the same variety and
spelling as the Catalan basics course. No readings (Latin script).

48 lessons in 9 units: A1 1–20, A2 21–39, B1 40–48.

## What makes Catalan Catalan (and what this course leans on)

- **The weak pronouns** (*em, et, es, el, la, ho, li, ens, us, els, hi, en*): very frequent, very Catalan,
  and the hardest thing for an English speaker. They come in from the first lesson as chunks (*em dic*,
  *m'agrada*, *hi ha*) and are then taken up one job at a time (a spiral, below).
- **The past with *anar***: *vaig anar* "I went" (*vaig* is "I go"). A surprise for English speakers and a
  false friend for anyone who knows Spanish. It is set up early (lesson 15, *vaig al cinema* "I'm going to
  the cinema") so that lesson 24 can turn it round.
- **The *perfet* for today** (*avui he treballat*), against the past with *anar* for yesterday.
- ***ser* and *estar*, used the Catalan way**: *ser* for where things are (*On és l'estació?*), *estar* for
  states that come and go (*estic cansat*). Not Spanish usage.
- **Articles everywhere**: with names (*en Pere, la Maria, l'Anna*) and with possessives (*la meva mare*).
- ***haver de*** for "must" (never *tenir que*), ***cal*** and ***no cal***.
- Everyday Catalan chunks a native really uses: *em sap greu*, *et ve de gust?*, *què et poso?*, *em fa mal*,
  *me'n vaig*, *d'acord*, the bell-quarter clock (*dos quarts de deu*).

## The spiral: where the big structures come back

- **The weak pronouns**: *em dic, et dius* (1) → *hi ha* (7) → *m'agrada*: *em, et, li, ens, us, els*
  (14) → *em llevo*: the same pronouns for oneself (17) → *el, la, ho* for things (22) → *en* for amounts
  (23) → *el vaig veure* with the past (28) → *hi he estat* (26) → after the imperative (34) → *em fa mal*
  (35) → *hi* and *en* for places and topics, *me'n vaig* (40) → two together (41).
- ***anar***: *vaig al cinema* "I'm going" (15) → *vaig anar* "I went" (24) → a story (28) → background
  with the imperfect (31) → reported (42).
- **The past**: the past with *anar* (24) → *perfet* for today (25) → experiences (26) → *fa* = ago (27) →
  a story in order (28) → the imperfect (30) → both together (31) → reasons in the past (38) → reported
  speech (42).
- ***ser* and *estar***: *soc de* (1) → *ser* in full (3) → *ser* for where (6) → *estar* for states (9) →
  *estar* + gerund (29) → *era/estava* (30) → *sigui, estigui* (43) → *fos* (48).
- **Wanting and asking**: *vull, voldria* + a thing (2) → *vull* + a verb (19) → *et ve de gust?* (21) →
  *podria, m'agradaria* (36) → *vull que vinguis* (43).
- ***haver***: *hi ha* (7) → *haver de* (20) → *he menjat* (25) → *hi havia* (30) → *hauries de* (47).
- ***fer***: *què fas?* (15) → *fa calor* (16) → *fa dos anys* (27) → *em fa mal* (35) → *faria*, *fes* (47–48).
- **Questions**: question words (5) → *on és?* (6) → *quant costa?* (8) → *quants anys tens?* (10) →
  *quina hora és?* (18) → *has estat mai…?* (26) → polite questions (36).
- **The future**: present + time word (21) → the future tense (37) → *quan* + subjunctive (46).
- **The conditional**: *voldria* as a chunk (2) → *podria* (36) → *hauries de, aniria* (47) → *si tingués…*
  (48).

## Choices

- *Ser* is taught for location (the standard, and what is heard in Barcelona for places: *On és el
  lavabo?*); *estic a casa* is mentioned as everyday speech too.
- The past with *anar* uses the central forms *vam, vau, van* (*vàrem, vàreu, varen* are mentioned only
  as forms one may hear).
- "Going to do" is not taught as *anar a* + infinitive (avoided in standard Catalan for the future): plans
  are the present with a time word, *pensar* + infinitive, or the future tense. *Vaig a comprar pa* is
  taught only with its real movement meaning, as the contrast to *vaig comprar pa*.
- Personal article: *en* (men), *la* (women), *l'* before a vowel; *na* is mentioned as Balearic and formal.
- The clock: both *les cinc i quart* (everyday) and the traditional *un quart de sis* (very common in
  Barcelona) are taught.
