# Russian → English: the plan

American English taught to a Russian speaker, with every explanation written in natural Russian, for
that speaker (not translated from an English course). From zero to everyday conversation (A2, touching
B1). The examples are American English (apartment, mom, movie, vacation, subway, check); the test
`tests/js/grammar-ru.test.mjs` flags British words and spellings, and Cyrillic inside English.

## What is hard for a Russian speaker, and so gets the most time

- **a / the**: Russian has no articles. They come early (lesson 3) and come back all through the course:
  a + job (I'm a teacher), plurals with no article, there is a…, generic nouns with none (I like coffee),
  the + unique things and some place names, the + superlatives, uncountable nouns with none.
- **"be" must be said**: Я студент → I'm a student; Мне 25 → I'm 25; Мне холодно → I'm cold;
  Холодно → It's cold (a sentence always has a subject).
- **One Russian present, two English ones**: I work / I'm working, from A1.
- **do / does / did** in questions and negatives; the -s of he / she / it.
- **Tense is not aspect**: the past simple vs the present perfect (have you ever, just, yet), and the
  classic "Я живу здесь пять лет" → I've lived here for five years. Past continuous for background,
  past perfect in stories.
- **Fixed word order** does the work of Russian's cases: who did what to whom is in the order, not the
  endings. Questions invert; indirect questions don't (Do you know where the station is?). Adverbs of
  frequency before the verb; "I really like", never "I very like".
- **there is / there are** where Russian starts with the place (В комнате есть стол).
- **Prepositions** that don't match (listen to, wait for, at home, on Monday) or that English doesn't use
  (call someone, discuss something, answer a question).
- **No double negatives**: I don't know anybody (Я никого не знаю).
- **if / when + present** for the future (If it rains, we'll stay home), where Russian uses the future.
- **Reported speech moves the tense back** (He said he was tired), where Russian keeps it.
- **"I agree"**, not "I am agree"; "I don't think he'll come", not "I think he won't come".
- Pronunciation notes in tips: th, w vs v, h (softer than х), long and short vowels (sheep / ship), and
  final voiced consonants (bag / back: Russian devoices them).
- Capital letters for days, months, languages and nationalities, and for I.

## The spiral: where each big structure comes back

| Structure | First | Comes back in |
|---|---|---|
| be | say-who-you-are, first-questions | numbers-and-age, weather-and-feelings, was-and-were, right-now (am working), passive |
| articles | say-who-you-are (a + job), a-and-the | more-than-one, there-is, like-and-want, places-and-going, general-or-specific, compare (the best), how-much-how-many, who-which-that |
| do | questions-with-do | negatives-with-dont, past-irregular (did), nothing-and-nobody, keep-talking (So do I) |
| present simple / continuous | every-day, right-now | going-to (arrangements), how-often, lately |
| past | was-and-were | past-regular, past-irregular, past-continuous, used-to, reported-speech, tell-a-story |
| present perfect | have-you-ever | just-already-yet, for-and-since, passive (has been), lately |
| future | going-to, will | if-and-when, imagine (would) |
| word order | first-questions, questions-with-do | there-is, how-often, indirect-questions, reported-speech |
| prepositions | there-is (place) | time-and-days, places-and-going, verbs-and-prepositions, phrasal-verbs |
| politeness | ask-politely | can (Can you…?), will (Shall I…?), suggest, indirect-questions, imagine (I'd…) |
