# Italian → English grammar: the plan

American English taught to an Italian speaker; every explanation is written in natural Italian, for an
Italian reader (not translated from the English-explained courses). No readings (`"reading": ""`). The
spelling and the words are American (*vacation, apartment, store, cell phone, soccer, color*). 48 lessons
in 8 units: lessons 1–19 A1, 20–39 A2, 40–48 B1.

## What is hard for an Italian speaker, and how the order answers it

- **The subject is always said.** Italian leaves it out (*Sono stanco, Piove, È tardi*); English never
  does (*I'm tired, It's raining, It's late*). From lesson 1, and *it* for weather and time in lesson 3.
- **Where Italian says *avere*, English says *be***: *I'm 25, I'm hungry, I'm cold* (lesson 4); and *I was
  born* (24), not *I am born*.
- **The article before a job** (*She's a doctor*, Italian *è medico*: lesson 2); **no article for things in
  general** (*I like dogs, Life is short*, Italian *mi piacciono i cani*: lessons 7, 17).
- **Adjectives go before the noun and never take -s** (*two red cars*): lesson 7, and every description
  after it.
- ***his / her* follow the owner, not the thing** (Italian *suo/sua* agree with the thing): lesson 8.
- ***do / does* in questions and negatives**, with no equivalent in Italian: the centre of unit 3 (11–13),
  and back with *did* (27), *have* (14) and indirect questions (47).
- **The third-person -s** (*she works*): lesson 12, then in every lesson that uses the present.
- **Present simple vs continuous**: Italian *faccio* covers both. Lesson 19, then plans (22) and stories
  (28).
- **Present perfect vs passato prossimo**: the passato prossimo looks like the present perfect but is
  used like the past simple (*Ieri ho visto Marco* = *I saw Marco yesterday*). A unit of its own (6), after
  the past simple is solid.
- ***for / since* with the present perfect** where Italian has *da* + present (*Abito qui da tre anni* =
  *I've lived here for three years*): lesson 33.
- **Will in the if-clause** (*If it will rain* ✗, from Italian *se pioverà*) and **would in the if-clause**
  (*If I would have* ✗): lessons 39 and 44.
- ***I want you to come***, not *I want that you come* (Italian *voglio che tu venga*): lesson 48.
- ***make* or *do***, both *fare* (41); phrasal verbs (43); *I agree*, not *I am agree* (40).
- **Pronunciation notes** as tips where they matter: *h* (he, his, hungry: 2, 4, 8), *th* (this, that,
  think: 6), short and long vowels (*live / leave*: 11), *-ed* (25), *can / can't* (18), the final
  consonants (no *I'm-a*: 1).

## The spiral: where each big structure comes back (`from`)

- **be**: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 7 (descriptions) → 19 (*I'm working*) → 24 (*was, were*) → 46 (the passive).
- **the subject always said**: 1 → 3 (*It's raining*) → 9 (*there is*) → 12 (*she works*) → 33.
- **a / the / no article**: 2 (*a teacher*) → 5 → 6 (plurals) → 7 → 17 (*I like dogs*) → 21 → 36 (*the best*).
- **do / does / did**: 11 → 12 → 13 → 14 (*Do you have…?*) → 17 → 27 (*did*) → 47 (no *do* in indirect
  questions).
- **present simple vs continuous**: 11–13 → 19 → 22 (plans) → 28 (*was walking*) → 33 (*I've been
  learning*).
- **the past**: 24 → 25 → 26 → 27 → 28 → 29 → 31 (vs the present perfect) → 45 (reported speech).
- **the present perfect**: 30 → 31 → 32 → 33 → 36 (*the best I've ever seen*) → 46.
- **the future**: 22 (*going to*) → 34 (*will*) → 39 (*if… will*) → 44 (*would*).
- **modals**: 18 (*can*) → 23 (*could, would*) → 37 (*should*) → 38 (*have to, must*) → 44 (*would*) → 47
  (*Could you tell me…?*).
- **questions**: 3 (with *be*) → 13 (with *do*) → 27 (with *did*) → 30 (*Have you ever…?*) → 33 (*How
  long…?*) → 47 (indirect).
- **adjectives**: 7 → 35 (comparatives) → 36 (superlatives).

## Choices to note

- American usage throughout: *Can I get a coffee?* (normal in American cafés), *Do you have…?* more than
  *Have you got…?*, *I just ate* beside *I've just eaten* (lesson 32 says both are fine in the US).
- Grammar words are the Italian ones a reader knows from school (*soggetto, verbo, aggettivo*), defined in
  plain words the first time; the English names of tenses (*present perfect*) are given once, because
  Italian learners meet them everywhere.
