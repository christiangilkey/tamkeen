# English → Korean: the plan

Korean taught to an English speaker, in the polite **해요 style** (what adults say to people they don't
know well: shops, work, new friends). 합니다 is learned to recognise (announcements, news) and 반말
(casual speech with close friends) comes at B1. Examples are standard Seoul Korean in Hangul, with
Revised Romanization as the reading (`r`), sound changes inside a word included (합니다 *hamnida*,
같이 *gachi*), tensing not marked, as the official system does.

## What makes Korean Korean, and the order that follows from it

- **The verb comes last**, and everything about the sentence (polite or not, past or not, a question or
  not, "and", "because", "if") is said by the **ending** on the verb. So the course is, in the end, a
  course of endings, each one a tool for one job: -고 싶어요 to want, -았어요 to tell what happened,
  -(으)ㄹ까요? to suggest, -아서 to give a reason.
- **Particles** after a noun say what it does in the sentence: 은/는, 이/가, 을/를, 에, 에서, (으)로,
  하고, 도, 만. Each comes with the job it does, early, and comes back.
- **The final consonant (받침)** decides the shape of most particles and endings (이에요/예요, 은/는,
  을/를, -으세요/-세요). It is explained in lesson 1 and used in every lesson after.
- **Adjectives are verbs** (descriptive verbs): 비싸요 is "is expensive" all by itself.
- **Vowel harmony** (-아요/-어요) is the first conjugation, in lesson 7; before it, -고 싶어요 lets the
  learner use any verb just by its stem.
- **Politeness is grammar**: speech levels (해요, 합니다, 반말) and the honorific -시-.

A beginner needs first to say who they are, name things, ask for things, say what they have and where
things are, and say what they want: so unit 1 does exactly that with nouns, 이에요, 있어요, 주세요 and
-고 싶어요, before the first real conjugation.

## The spiral: where each big structure comes back

- **은/는 and 이/가** (the hardest pair): 1 (저는: "as for me"), 4 (이/가 with 있어요), 5 (은/는 for what
  you ask about), 10 (은/는 for contrast), 11 (누가: question words take 이/가), 12 (이/가 with
  descriptive verbs), 34 (contrast with -지만), 40 (이/가 inside a describing clause), 45 (new vs known
  in a story).
- **에 / 에서 / (으)로**: 5 (에 for where something is), 8 (에 "to" vs 에서 "at, doing"), 15 (에 for time),
  20 ((으)로 "by"), 28 (에서…까지, (으)로 "towards").
- **The present -아요/-어요** (7) is the base of -아서 (32, 35), -아/어 주세요 (16), -아/어야 (24),
  -아/어 보다 (38), -아/어지다 (47), the past -았/었어요 (17), and 반말 (50).
- **Stem + -고**: -고 싶어요 (6), -고 "and" (19), -고 있어요 (25), and the difference from -아서 (35).
- **The helping vowel 으** (16): -(으)세요, then -(으)ㄹ 거예요 (21), -(으)ㄹ까요 (22), -(으)ㄹ 수
  있어요 (23), -(으)면 (27), -(으)니까 (33), -(으)ㄴ 후에 (36), the modifiers (40, 41).
- **The past**: what you did (17), how it was (18), in a chain (19), with 안/못 (23), in a reason (32), in a
  modifier (41), in a story (45), reported (46).
- **The future and plans**: -(으)ㄹ 거예요 (21), -(으)ㄹ까요 (22), -(으)려고 해요 / -기로 했어요 (26),
  -(으)ㄹ게요 (31), the -(으)ㄹ modifier (41), -(으)ㄹ 것 같아요 (43).
- **Negation**: 안 and -지 않아요 (9), past (17), 못 (23), 안 -아/어도 돼요 (24), -지 마세요 (16).
- **Requests and politeness**: 주세요 (3), -(으)세요 / -아/어 주세요 (16), -(으)ㄹ까요 (22), -(으)시- (29),
  합니다 (30), 드릴게요 / 주시겠어요 (31), down to 반말 (50).
- **Questions**: yes/no by tone (2), question words (11), 몇 and 얼마 (13–14), -(으)ㄹ까요? (22), -죠?
  (39), reported questions -냐고 (46).
- **Reasons and "but"**: -아서 (32), -(으)니까 (33), -지만 (34), -는데 (44), -거든요 (49).
- **-(으)면**: as a chunk in 24 (-(으)면 안 돼요), taught in 27, used for directions (28) and wishes (48).

## Reading notes

Lesson 1 explains Hangul's syllable blocks and the final consonant; lesson 2 explains the Revised
Romanization spellings that surprise English speakers (eo, eu, ae/e, and why 학교 is *hakgyo*).
Practice options are in Hangul only (the format has no reading for them): reading them is good practice,
and every example above them has its reading.
