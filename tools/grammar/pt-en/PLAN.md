# Portuguese → American English: the plan

American English taught to a Brazilian, explained in natural Brazilian Portuguese (*você*), written for
that learner and comparing English with Portuguese only, never with a third language.

**What is hard for a Brazilian, and so gets time and comes back often:**
- **The subject is always said.** Portuguese drops it (*Está chovendo. É tarde. Sou professora.*);
  English can't: *It's raining. It's late. I'm a teacher.*
- **"a/an" with jobs** (*Ele é médico* → *He's a doctor*) and **no "the" for things in general**
  (*Eu gosto de café / A vida é curta* → *I like coffee / Life is short*).
- **"do / does / did"** in questions and negatives, which Portuguese has no word for; the **-s** of
  *he works*.
- **"there is / there are"**, never *have* (*Tem muita gente* → *There are a lot of people*).
- **Adjectives before the noun, never plural** (*carros azuis* → *blue cars*).
- **Age and birth with "be"**: *I'm 25* (not *I have 25 years*), *I was born in 1999* (*nasci*).
- **his / her** chosen by the owner, not by the thing (*seu/sua* follow the thing).
- **The present perfect**, which Portuguese doesn't use the same way: *Você já foi…?* → *Have you ever
  been…?*; *Moro aqui há dois anos* → *I've lived here for two years*.
- **in / on / at** (all *em*), **make / do** (*fazer*), **say / tell** (*dizer*).
- **"When I get home"**, not *when I will get* (Portuguese uses the future subjunctive: *quando eu chegar*).
- **False friends**: *pretend, actually, push, library, parents, realize, eventually, attend*.
- **Pronunciation notes** where they belong: no extra "i" after final consonants (*Facebook*, *hot dog*),
  *th*, the *h* of *house* vs the Brazilian *r*, the -s and -ed endings, *can / can't*.

About 40% A1 (lessons 1–20), 40% A2 (21–39), 20% B1 (40–50).

## The spiral

- **be**: *I'm* (1) → jobs (2) → *not* and questions (3) → *It's a…* (4) → *How old…?* (6) → description
  (7) → *was/were* (29) → *I was born* (29, 49) → the passive (49).
- **The subject always said / it**: *I'm* (1) → *It's cold* (3) → *It's a book* (4) → *I like it* (17)
  → objects *it* (25) → *It's raining* (19).
- **do / does / did**: as a chunk (*What do you do?*, 2) → *don't* (14) → questions (15) → *have* (16) →
  *like* (17) → *How often do you…?* (18) → *did* (28) → *So do I* (50).
- **There is**: (8) → *there was / were* (29) → *there's going to be* (21) … and *have* (16) kept apart.
- **a / the / no article**: jobs (2) → *a / an / the* (4, 5) → generic nouns (17) → uncountables (37).
- **in / on / at**: places (9) → times (12) → *on the bus, at home* (24) → phrasal verbs (45).
- **Tenses**: present simple (13–18) → continuous (19) → *going to* (21) → *will* (22) → past (26–31) →
  present perfect (32–34) → *will* in *if* sentences (41) → *would* (42) → reported (48).
- **Questions**: *be* questions (3, 6) → *do* questions (15) → *did* (28) → *Have you ever…?* (32) →
  *How long have you…?* (33) → tag questions (50).

From lesson 4 on, every lesson has at least one practice question marked `from` an earlier lesson.

## As written
50 lessons: 20 A1 (1–20), 19 A2 (21–39), 11 B1 (40–50). Every piece of English inside the Portuguese
explanations (summaries, questions, tips, table labels) is marked `{{ }}`, so the app shows and speaks it
as English. The right option of every choice, fill and dialogue reply sits at a varied place.
