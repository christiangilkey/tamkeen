# English → Italian grammar: the plan

Italian (Italy), standard modern Italian as spoken across the country, explained in English. No readings
(`"reading": ""`): Italian is written in Latin letters, and accents are part of the spelling (è, perché,
più, città). 48 lessons in 8 units: lessons 1–19 A1, 20–39 A2, 40–48 B1.

## What makes Italian hard for an English speaker, and how the order answers it

- **Every noun is masculine or feminine, and the articles change with the next sound** (il, lo, la, l',
  i, gli, le; un, uno, una, un'). Taught in three small steps (lessons 3, 6, 8), each tied to something
  to do (order, ask where, talk about more than one), then joined to prepositions (nel, sul, al) in 12–13.
- **Adjectives agree** (una casa bianca, i vini rossi): from lesson 7, and in every description after.
- **The verb ending says who** (parlo, parli, parla…), so "I" and "you" are usually left out. First with
  essere (1), then -are verbs (4), -ere/-ire (15), the irregulars that matter (andare 12, fare/uscire/
  venire 18, potere/dovere/volere/sapere 20).
- **Where Italian uses a different verb from English**: *avere* for age, hunger, cold (9); *c'è* for
  "there is" (10); *fare* for weather (18); *mi piace*, back to front (16).
- **Tu or Lei** from lesson 2, and back with every new verb form (questions 5, *Le piace* 16, *Può…?* 20,
  *Potrebbe…?* 38, *Vada, giri* 40).
- **Two auxiliaries in the past** (ho mangiato, sono andata) and **two pasts** (passato prossimo for
  events, imperfetto for background and habits): unit 5, a spiral of seven lessons, then back for
  reported speech (45) and the trapassato (47).
- **Da + present** for something still going on (Studio italiano da due anni): its own lesson (27),
  because English uses a perfect tense there.
- **Little pronouns before the verb** (lo, gli, ne, ci, me lo): built up from *mi piace* (16) and
  reflexives (21) through unit 6 to joined pronouns (46).
- **The congiuntivo** comes in through a back door: the polite *Lei* commands (*vada, prenda*, 40) are
  its forms, so opinions (41) and wishes (42) reuse them; the imperfect subjunctive closes the course with
  imagining (48).

## The spiral: where each big structure comes back (`from`)

- **essere**: 1 → 7 (descriptions) → 8 (plural) → 25 (as the past's helper) → 41 (*sia*) → 48 (*fossi*).
- **articles and gender**: 3 (un/una) → 6 (il/la) → 8 (i/gli/le) → 12–13 (al, nel, sul) → 16 (*mi piace
  il…*) → 19 (possessives keep the article) → 34–35 (*del*, *il più*).
- **tu / Lei**: 2 → 5 → 16 (*Le piace*) → 20 (*Può…?*) → 31 (*Le*) → 38 (*Potrebbe…?*) → 40 (*vada*).
- **the present**: 4 → 15 → 18 → 20 → 21 → 22 → 27 (*da* + present) → 37 (*se* + present).
- **wanting**: 3 (*vorrei*) → 20 (*voglio*) → 38 (the condizionale) → 42 (*voglio che*) → 48.
- **the past**: 23 → 24 → 25 → 26 → 27 → 28 → 29 → 30 (*l'ho preso*) → 45 (*ha detto che*) → 47 (*era
  già partito*).
- **little pronouns**: 16 (*mi piace*) → 21 (*mi alzo*) → 30 → 31 → 32 (*ne*) → 33 (*ci*, from *c'è* 10) →
  39 (*dimmi, prendilo*) → 46 (*me lo, glielo*).
- **questions**: 5 → 6 (*dov'è*) → 9 (*quanti anni hai*) → 11 (*quanto costa*) → 14 (*a che ora*) → 27
  (*da quanto tempo*) → 33 (*ci sei mai stato?*).
- **the congiuntivo**: 40 (*vada, prenda*: the same forms) → 41 → 42 → 48 (*se avessi*).
- **the future and conditions**: 18 (the present for plans) → 36 → 37 → 38 → 48.

## Choices to note

- *Lei* (polite "you") is written with a capital letter, as most courses for learners do.
- Where everyday speech often uses the indicative instead of the congiuntivo (*penso che è*), the
  lessons teach the standard form and say that the other one is heard.
- The passato remoto is not taught: in most of Italy it lives in books, and a learner needs to recognise
  it only when reading stories (a tip in lesson 47).
