# Spanish → English (es-en): the plan

American English taught to a Spanish speaker, explanations in natural **Mexican Spanish** (the learner
is *tú*; words such as *celular, carro, platicar, departamento*), written for that speaker, not translated
from the English-explained courses.

## What is hard for a Spanish speaker, and what is easy
Easy: the alphabet, most word order (subject, verb, object), many shared words (*hospital, important,
family*), the idea of tenses and of a present perfect. No gender to learn.

Hard, and so given the most time and the most mistakes to watch for:
- **The subject is always said** (*It's raining*, *It's late*, *She's a doctor*), because the verb hardly
  changes to show who.
- ***do* in questions and negatives** (*Do you work here?*, *I don't know*): Spanish has nothing like it.
  It comes in unit 2 and back in every unit (*did* in unit 4, *Does she…?* everywhere).
- **The third-person -s** (*she works*), easy to forget because it is the only ending.
- **Adjectives before the noun and never plural** (*two big houses*).
- **be where Spanish uses tener**: *I'm 20*, *I'm hungry*, *I'm cold*, *I'm right*.
- ***a/an* with jobs** (*She's a nurse*), and **no *the* for things in general** (*I love dogs*).
- **Present simple vs continuous** (*I work* / *I'm working*), and state verbs (*I want*, not *I'm wanting*).
- **Irregular pasts** and ***did* + base form** (*Did you go?*, not *Did you went?*).
- **The present perfect with *for/since*** (*I've lived here for five years*, not *I live here since…*),
  and where Mexican Spanish uses the preterite (*¿Ya comiste?* = *Did you eat yet?* in the US).
- ***in/on/at*** for *en*.
- ***make/do/take/have***, phrasal verbs, ***-ing* after prepositions** (*before leaving*), ***want you
  to*** (not *want that you*), ***If/when* + present** (where Spanish uses the subjunctive).
- Uncountable nouns that are countable in Spanish (*information, advice, furniture, news*).
- False friends (*embarrassed, actually, realize, attend, library, career*).
- Pronunciation notes where they matter for grammar: the -s and -ed endings, *e* before *s* (*Spain*, not
  *Espain*), short and long vowels (*live/leave*).

## The order
Unit 1 says who you are with *be* (and every Spanish-speaker trap of *be*: the subject, *a* + job, *I'm 20*).
Unit 2 is the present simple, built in the order the problems appear: the plain form, then the -s, then
*don't/doesn't*, then *Do…?*. Unit 3 goes out: *there is*, *in/on/at*, *can*, the continuous and plans.
Unit 4 is the past (*was/were*, -ed, irregulars, *did*, the past continuous for stories, *used to* for
the Spanish imperfect of habits). Unit 5: the future with *will*, comparing, quantities, advice and
obligation. Unit 6: the present perfect (experiences, *for/since*, news), *make/do* and phrasal verbs.
Unit 7 joins ideas: *-ing* or *to*, relative clauses, *if/when*, *want you to*. Unit 8: imagination,
opinions, reported speech, feelings and false friends, the passive, keeping a conversation going.

## The spiral: where each big structure comes back
- **be**: 1, 2, 3 → 5 → 13 (*there is*) → 16 (*am/is/are* + -ing) → 19 (*It's*) → 20 (*was/were*) → 24 → 46
  (passive).
- **do / does / did**: 9, 10 → 11 (*Do you like…?*) → 12 (*Do you have…?*) → 17 → 23 (*did*) → 47 (*So do I*).
- **the -s**: 8 → 9 (*doesn't* without -s) → 12 (*has*) → 17 → 39.
- **the past**: 20 → 21, 22, 23 → 24 → 25 → 34 (vs present perfect) → 43 (reported speech) → 48.
- **present perfect**: 32 → 33 → 34 → 48.
- **the future**: 18 (*going to*) → 26 (*will*) → 39 (*if* + present, *will*) → 48.
- **can / could / would**: 6 (*Can I get…? I'd like*) → 15 → 31 → 40 (*Could you…?*) → 41 (*would*).
- **in/on/at**: 14 → 18, 20 (*born in*), 22, 28 (*in the world*).
- **questions**: 3 → 10 → 13 → 23 → 32 (*Have you ever…?*) → 33 (*How long…?*) → 47.
- **adjectives**: 5 → 27, 28 (comparing) → 44 (*-ed/-ing*).

## Conventions
- Examples are natural American English (*apartment, check, cell phone, vacation, downtown, a quarter
  after five*), US spelling. Translations are natural Mexican Spanish.
- Speakers' names in dialogues are in Spanish (*Mesero*, *Tú*) since they are explanation text.
- `order` questions use sentences whose order is fixed; punctuation stays on the last word.
- `tests/js/grammar-es.test.mjs` checks the Spanish explanations (¿ ¡ pairs, accents on question words,
  common missing accents) and the English (no ¿ ¡, no British spellings).
