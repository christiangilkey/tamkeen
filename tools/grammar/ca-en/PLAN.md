# Catalan → English: the plan

American English taught to a Catalan speaker, explanations written in natural Catalan (central, IEC 2017
spelling), for someone who has never studied grammar. The comparisons are always with Catalan. No
readings.

48 lessons in 9 units: A1 1–20, A2 21–39, B1 40–48.

## What is hard for a Catalan speaker (and gets the most time)

- **The subject is always said**: *Plou* → *It's raining*; *És tard* → *It's late*; *Soc a casa* → *I'm
  home*. Comes back in almost every lesson.
- ***do* in questions and negatives**: *Parles anglès?* → *Do you speak English?*; *No ho sé* → *I don't
  know*. Then *did* in the past.
- **One verb *be* for *ser* and *estar*, and *be* where Catalan has *tenir***: *I'm 30*, *I'm hungry*, *I'm
  cold*, *you're right*.
- **Articles**: *a* with jobs (*I'm a teacher*: *Soc professor*); no *the* with things in general (*I like
  coffee*: *M'agrada el cafè*); *my* with body parts (*I hurt my hand*: *M'he fet mal a la mà*).
- ***his/her* go with the owner**, not with the thing (*el seu / la seva* go with the thing).
- **Present simple vs present continuous** (Catalan's present does both: *Ara treballo*).
- **Present perfect vs *perfet***: the *perfet* is used for today (*Aquest matí he vist la Maria*); English
  uses the past for any finished time (*I saw Maria this morning*), and the present perfect for *for/since*
  (*Fa dos anys que visc aquí* → *I've lived here for two years*).
- **"going to" looks like *vaig a*** but *vaig* + infinitive is the Catalan past: a two-way trap.
- **Word order**: adjective before the noun and never plural (*red cars*); *I like coffee very much*, not
  *I like very much coffee*; *always* before the verb; object pronouns after the verb (*I see her*, not *I
  her see*, as *la veig*).
- **After a preposition, *-ing***: *abans de sortir* → *before leaving*; *gràcies per venir* → *thanks for
  coming*.
- ***want someone to***: *Vull que vinguis* → *I want you to come* (never *I want that you come*).
- ***I agree*** (not *I am agree*), *it depends on*, *married to*, *think about*, *dream about*.
- **Uncountable nouns** that are countable in Catalan: *information, advice, news, furniture, homework*.
- **False friends**, one or two where they fit: *actually, library, large, exit, embarrassed, carpet,
  sensible, assist/attend, realize, parents, constipated*.
- **Pronunciation**, as short tips in the lessons where the sound first matters: the *h* of *hello*, *th*,
  *v* vs *b* (central Catalan has no *v* sound), the *e* Catalan speakers add before *s* + consonant
  (*Spain*, not *Espain*), short and long vowels (*ship/sheep*, *live/leave*), the three *-ed* endings,
  *-s* endings, the weak vowel of *a, the, to* (Catalan's neutral vowel helps here).
- **Writing**: *I* is always a capital; days, months, languages and nationalities too (*Monday, May,
  English, Catalan*).

## The spiral: where the big structures come back

- ***be***: *I'm, I'm from* (1) → *am, is, are* (2) → *it's* (3) → questions with *be* (6) → *there is* (7) →
  *I'm 30, I'm hungry* (10) → *be* + *-ing* (17) → *going to* (21) → *was, were* (23) → *was doing* (27) →
  *If I were you* (46) → the passive (48).
- ***do***: the verb *do* does nothing alone (13) → *Do you like…?* (14) → *What time do you…?* (15–16) →
  simple vs continuous (17) → *did* (26) → *didn't use to* (32) → *I don't think* (45).
- **The subject is always said**: *I'm* (1) → *it's raining* (3) → *there is* (7) → every lesson's
  mistakes, especially 12, 17 and 24.
- **Articles**: *a* with jobs (2) → *a/an* (4) → *a, the*, plurals (5) → no *the* for things in general (14) →
  *a* with illnesses (38).
- **The present**: present simple (12) → *do* (13) → present continuous (17) → continuous for
  arrangements (21) → present after *if* and *when* (40).
- **The past**: *was/were* (23) → *-ed* (24) → irregular (25) → *did* (26) → past continuous (27) → a story
  (28) → *used to* (32) → past vs present perfect (34) → *ago* (35) → reported speech (47) → *was built* (48).
- **The present perfect**: experiences (33) → today vs yesterday (34) → *for, since* (35) → *I've had a
  headache since Monday* (38).
- **The future**: *going to* (21) → *will* (36) → first conditional (40).
- **Modal verbs**: *can* (18) → *have to* (19) → *let's* (20) → *should, must* (37) → *would* (46).
- **Object pronouns and word order**: *like it* (14) → *me, him, her, it* (22) → phrasal verbs, *turn it
  off* (43) → *want you to* (44) → *told me* (47).
- ***-ing***: *I like swimming* (14) → present continuous (17) → past continuous (27) → after a preposition
  (41).
- **Questions**: with *be* (6) → *Is there…?* (7) → *How much…?* (9) → *do* (13) → *Can I…?* (18) → *did* (26)
  → *Have you ever…?* (33) → *How long have you…?* (35).

## Choices

- American English throughout: *apartment, movie, vacation, cell phone, check* (the bill), *store*,
  *gotten*; *I have* rather than *I've got* (both shown); the present perfect taught as standard, with a
  note that Americans often use the past with *just, already, yet* (*I already ate*).
- Explanations compare only with Catalan, never with Spanish.
- Grammar words are the ones a Catalan school uses (*verb, subjecte, complement, temps verbal*), each
  explained in plain words the first time.
