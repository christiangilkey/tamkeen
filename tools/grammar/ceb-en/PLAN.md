# Bisaya → English: the plan

American English, explained in natural Bisaya (Cebu) for a Bisaya speaker who knows no grammar. The
explanations compare with Bisaya, never with a third language, and use the spelling of the Bisaya basics
course (`og`, `gyud`, `dinhi`, `ganahan`, `selpon`). Grammar words are given in Bisaya with the English
school word beside them (`berbo (verb)`), because that is how Filipinos meet them at school.

## What is hard for a Bisaya speaker (what gets the most time)

1. **"Be" has to be said**: `Maestra siya.` → *She **is** a teacher.* Bisaya has no word for it.
2. **He or she**: `siya` is both. English must choose *he/she* (and *his/her*, *him/her*); things and
   animals are *it*. The commonest mistake of all (*My mother, he…*), so it comes back in many lessons.
3. **Time, not "done / not yet"**: Bisaya verbs say whether an action is done (`mi-`), not yet (`mo-`) or
   going on (`nag-`); English verbs say **when**. `nag-` alone can be *I'm cooking / I cooked / I was
   cooking*; `mo-` can be *I'll go* or *I go (every Sunday)*. Each English tense is taught against the
   Bisaya form it splits.
4. **Do** in questions and *no*: `Ganahan ka og kape?` → *Do you like coffee?*; `Dili ko…` → *I don't…*.
5. **The subject comes first**, before the verb (Bisaya starts with the verb or the describing word).
6. **a / the / nothing**: `ang` is used far more than *the* (`Lami ang lechon.` → *Lechon is delicious.*);
   *a* where Bisaya has nothing (`Maestra siya.` → *She's **a** teacher.*).
7. **Plural -s and the -s of he/she**: `mga` is often left out and numbers need no `mga`; English always
   marks *two book**s***, *she work**s***.
8. **in / on / at** (and *to, from*) for the one word `sa`.
9. **Philippine English habits** that American listeners notice: *open/close the light* → *turn on/off*;
   *borrow* vs *lend* (`hulam` is both); *How old are you?* not *How many years…*; answering negative
   questions (`Wala ka mokaon?` `Oo.` → *No, I didn't.*).
10. **Sounds**: f/v (not p/b), th, short and long vowels (*ship/sheep*), final -s and -ed: as tips.

## The spiral (where each big structure comes back)

- ***be***: 1 → 2 (*he/she is*) → 3 (questions) → 4 (*not*) → 7 (*there is*) → 8 (where) → 11 (describing)
  → 16 (*I'm cooking*) → 20 (*was/were*) → 25 (*was cooking*) → 34 (feelings) → 45 (passive).
- ***he / she / it***: 2 → 6 (*his/her*) → 14 (*she works*) → 22, 24 (stories) → 40 (*who / which*) → 43 (reported).
- **Tense vs Bisaya aspect**: present simple 13–15 → continuous 16 (`nag-` split) → past 20–24 → past continuous 25
  → *going to / will* 26–27 (`mo-` split) → present perfect 35–36, 38 → *used to* 39 → conditionals 41–42 → reported 43.
- ***do***: *don't* 12–13 → *doesn't* 14 → questions 15 → *did* 23 → *Why don't we…?* 28 → *don't have to* 29.
- **Questions**: 3 → 10 (*how much/many*) → 15 → 23 → 35 (*Have you ever…?*) → 36 (*yet*) → 38 (*How long…?*).
- ***a / the***: *a/an* 5 → *a* then *the* 7 → *an* + adjective 11 → no *the* for things in general 17 → *some/any* 33.
- **Plural -s**: 5 → 10 → 17 → 33.
- ***in / on / at***: 8 → *at* + time 13 → *on Monday, in May* 21 → movement 30.
- **Commands**: 19 → 28 → 30.
- **Philippine English habits**: *turn on the light* 19 → *borrow/lend* 18 → *How old…?* 10 → negative questions 4, 23 → 44.
