# German → English: the plan

American English explained in German, for a German speaker who has never studied grammar. The
explanations are written in natural German, addressing the learner as *du* (as German learning apps
do), and they compare with German wherever that helps: German and English are close cousins, so
much is easy, and the hard parts are exactly where German does something else. 48 lessons in 9
units: 19 A1, 19 A2, 10 B1.

## What is hard for a German speaker (and so decides the order and the mistakes)

- **The progressive.** German has one present (*ich arbeite*); English has two (*I work* / *I'm
  working*). It comes early (unit 4), is contrasted with the simple present at once, and comes back for
  the past (*I was cooking when…*), for plans (*I'm meeting Tom tomorrow*) and with the present perfect
  (*I've been waiting for an hour*).
- **do.** German makes questions and negatives by moving the verb or adding *nicht*; English needs *do*
  (*Do you live here? I don't know.*). Taught in unit 2, then for the past (*Did you…?*).
- **SVO in every clause.** No verb second after a time word (*Yesterday I went…*, not *Yesterday went
  I…*), no verb last after *because, that, if* or in indirect questions (*Could you tell me where the
  station is?*). Taught with frequency words and time words (unit 4), then with clauses (unit 8).
- **Present perfect vs Perfekt.** They look alike and are used very differently: English uses the past
  simple for any finished time (*I saw him yesterday*), and the present perfect for experience, news
  and anything still going on (*I've lived here for five years* = *Ich wohne seit fünf Jahren hier*).
  A whole unit, plus *since/for* (not *since five years*).
- **will vs wollen**, **must not vs muss nicht**, **become vs bekommen**, **also, actual, eventually,
  chef, handy**: false friends, shown where they bite and gathered in one lesson.
- Small things that German speakers get wrong for years: *I'm a teacher* (with *a*); *bigger than* (not
  *as*); *-s* in *she works*; *information, advice, news* (no plural); *half past seven* (not *half
  eight*); adverbs in *-ly*; *If it rains* (not *If it will rain*).
- Pronunciation notes: *th*, *w* vs *v*, the voiced endings (*bed* vs *bet*, *dogs*), *-ed*.

## The spiral (where each big structure comes back)

- **do**: 6 (questions) → 7 (negatives) → 8 (*Do you have…?*) → 14 (*Do you usually…?*) → 21 (*did*) →
  46/47 (*So do I*, *don't you?*).
- **The continuous**: 13 → 14 (vs the simple present) → 22 (the past continuous) → 23 (plans) → 48
  (*I've been waiting*).
- **SVO word order**: 4–5 (the basic sentence) → 17 (frequency and time words) → 36 (*because I have*)
  → 38 (relative clauses) → 39 (indirect questions) → 43 (reported questions).
- **The past and the perfect**: 18–19 (*was, -ed*) → 20–21 (irregular, *did*) → 22 (stories) → 27–30
  (present perfect) → 40 (*used to*) → 43 (reported speech) → 44 (*was built*) → 48.
- **The future**: 23 (*going to*, arrangements) → 24 (*will*) → 37 (*If it rains, we'll…*) → 41
  (*would*).
- **Modal verbs**: 9 (*Can I have…?*) → 11 (*can*) → 25 (*shall*) → 26 (*have to, must*) → 41
  (*would, could*) → 42 (*should*).
- **Questions**: 2 (*wh-* with *be*) → 3 (yes/no) → 6 (*do*) → 21 (*did*) → 27 (*Have you ever…?*) → 29
  (*How long have you…?*) → 39 (indirect) → 47 (question tags).
- **False friends**: 24 (*will*) → 26 (*must not*) → 29 (*since*) → 34 (*become*) → 35 (all together) →
  37 (*when/if*).

Every lesson from the fourth on has at least one practice question with `from` (an earlier lesson's
grammar in the new situation).
