# Japanese → English grammar course: the plan

**Status: written (45 lessons), kept as a draft** (`"draft": true`: checked by the build and tests, not
published). The owner's plan (2026-10-25): courses for English speakers now, English for speakers of other
languages in the next update. When he says, remove `"draft": true` and run the build.

American English taught to a Japanese speaker. The explanations are written in natural Japanese
(です/ます, plain and friendly, the way a good teacher writes for a beginner), not translated from
English: they compare with Japanese, and the mistakes shown are the ones Japanese speakers really make.
No readings (`"reading": ""`): the learner reads Latin letters. Many learners did some English at school,
but the course assumes no grammar knowledge: each grammar word (主語, 動詞, 冠詞…) is explained when first
needed.

## What is hard for a Japanese speaker, and so gets most time
- **a / the, and plurals.** Japanese has neither. They start in lesson 2 (a/an with jobs), come back in
  4 (plurals), 10 (I like dogs: general nouns), 13 (there is a / some), 14 (a first, then the), have a
  lesson of their own (17), and come back with amounts (21).
- **The subject is always said**, even when Japanese leaves it out: "I'm from Osaka", "It's raining",
  "I wash **my** hands". From lesson 1, then 7 (possessives), 11 (empty "it").
- **Word order**: subject, verb, object (I eat rice, not I rice eat), and prepositions *before* the noun
  (in Tokyo, not Tokyo in). Relative clauses go *after* the noun (40), the opposite of Japanese.
- **be** must be said with adjectives too: "It **is** delicious" (Japanese おいしい needs no です-like
  verb to be a sentence).
- **Tense and aspect**: the present simple vs continuous (18), the past (23–26), the present perfect
  (35–37), where Japanese ～ている covers several of them: 知っている = I know, 3年住んでいる = I have lived
  here for three years.
- **Answers to negative questions** (27): "Don't you like it?" "**Yes**, I do." / "**No**, I don't." The
  opposite of はい / いいえ.
- **too / either** (22): Japanese も is both.
- **Politeness** is in modals and wording (Could you…?, I'd like…), not in verb endings (19); "You'd
  better" is much stronger than ～したほうがいい (33).
- **Pronunciation notes** where they matter: l / r, th, v / b, final consonants without an added vowel
  (dog, not "doggu"), can / can't, the -ed and -s endings.
- **Katakana English**: false friends where they bite (マンション ≠ mansion, クレーム ≠ claim).

## The English spine
Every English course covers: be; a/the and plurals; present simple with do; present continuous; can;
there is/are; have; past simple (regular and irregular); going to and will; comparatives and
superlatives; present perfect (ever, never, for, since, just, yet); should, have to, must; first and
second conditionals; relative clauses; used to; phrasal verbs; the passive to recognise. All are here.

## Units and lessons

Levels: lessons 1–19 A1, 20–36 A2, 37–45 B1 (42% / 38% / 20%).
