# Chinese → English (zh-en): the plan

American English taught to a speaker of Mandarin Chinese. The explanations are written in natural
simplified Chinese, the way a good Chinese teacher of English explains to an adult beginner (not
translated from English). Examples are American English with a Chinese translation; no readings.

## What is hard for a Chinese speaker, and the order that follows from it

Chinese has no verb endings, no articles, no plural endings and one spoken word, tā, for he, she and it.
English puts all of that into almost every sentence. So:

1. **be from the first lesson** (unit 1), and above all **be with adjectives**: *She is tall*, where
   Chinese says 她很高 without 是. *he* and *she* (他 / 她 sound the same, so speakers mix them) come
   back in nearly every lesson. *a / an* with jobs comes early.
2. **Plurals and articles** (unit 2): *two books* (never *two book*), *a cup of*, uncountable nouns, big
   numbers (一万 = *ten thousand*).
3. **The present simple with do** (unit 3): the third-person **-s** (the commonest mistake of all), *don't
   / doesn't*, *Do you…?*, and putting **time and place at the end** (*I work in Beijing*, Chinese puts them
   before the verb). *very* never goes before a verb (*I really like it*, not *I very like*).
4. **Places and time** (unit 4): prepositions after the thing (*on the table*, 在桌子上), **there is**
   (Chinese 有 does both "have" and "there is"), *at / on / in*, dates small to big.
5. **Now and next** (unit 5): *am / is / are + -ing*, *going to*, *will*, polite requests.
6. **The past** (unit 6): every verb must change (Chinese can leave the verb alone and say 昨天); *-ed*,
   irregular verbs, *did*, *used to*.
7. **Comparing and describing** (unit 7): *-er / more*, *the -est*, adverbs at the end (他英语说得很好 →
   *He speaks English very well*), *much / many*, and a lesson only on *a* and *the*.
8. **The present perfect** (unit 8), which has no single Chinese match: *ever / never* (过), *for / since*
   (住了三年了), *just / already / yet* (了); then *should*, *have to*, *must*.
9. **Connecting and telling** (unit 9, B1): **one** linking word (*because* without *so*, *although*
   without *but*), conditionals, relative clauses **after** the noun (*the book I bought*, 我买的书),
   the past continuous in a story, reported speech, phrasal verbs, the passive (used far more than 被),
   and answers to negative questions (*No, I don't*, where Chinese says 对，我不…).

## The spiral (practice with `from`)

- **be**: 1 (am / are) → 2 (with adjectives) → 3 (questions) → 5 (*I'm 25*) → 17 (where) → 21 (*-ing*) →
  22 (*going to*) → 25 (*was / were*) → 47 (the passive).
- **he / she / his / her**: 2 → 4 → 10 → 12 (*-s*) → 45 (*She told me*).
- **a / an / the and plurals**: 4 (*a* + job) → 6 (*these*) → 7 (-s) → 8 (uncountable) → 18 (*there's a…*)
  → 33 (*much / many*) → 34 (*a* vs *the*).
- **do**: 13 (*don't*) → 14 (*Do you…?*) → 15 (*Do you have…?*) → 16 → 28 (*did*) → 48 (*Don't you…?*) →
  49 (*So do I*).
- **-s on the verb**: 12 → 13 (*doesn't* + verb, no -s) → 15 (*has*) → 20 (*can*, no -s) → 35 (*has been*).
- **Time and place at the end**: 11 → 17 → 19 → 22 → 25.
- **The past**: 25 → 26 → 27 → 28 → 29 → 37 (vs the present perfect) → 44 (a story) → 45 (reported).
- **Questions with a helping word first**: 3 (*Are you…?*) → 5 → 14 (*Do you…?*) → 20 (*Can you…?*) →
  28 (*Did you…?*) → 35 (*Have you ever…?*) → 45 (*He asked if…*) → 49 (follow-ups).
- **Future and requests**: 8 (*I'd like*) → 20 (*Can I…?*) → 22 → 23 → 24 → 41 (*If…, I'll…*).
- **Comparing**: 30 → 31 → 42 (*I wish…*, imagining).
- **Linking**: 11 (*and*) → 40 → 41 → 43 → 44.

## Conventions

- American spelling and usage (*color, apartment, gas station, I'd like, fall*).
- Explanations: simplified characters, full-width Chinese punctuation; English inside `{{ }}`.
- Pronunciation notes (*th*, *v / w*, *l / n*, final consonants, the endings *-s* and *-ed*, no extra
  vowel after a consonant: *dog*, not *dog-e*) as tips where the lesson needs them.
