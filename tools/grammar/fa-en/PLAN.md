# Persian → English grammar: the plan

American English taught to a speaker of Persian (Iranian), explanations written in natural, simple Persian
(standard written Persian, as a good teacher writes for a learner: short sentences, everyday words, the
usual Persian grammar words: فعل، فاعل، مفعول، صفت، ضمیر، حرف اضافه). No readings (`"reading": ""`): the
learner reads Latin letters, and tapping an example lets them hear it.

## What is hard for a Persian speaker, and so gets the time

- **Word order.** Persian puts the verb last (من هر روز به مدرسه می‌روم); English puts it right after the
  subject (I go to school every day), and puts time and place at the end. From lesson 1, and in every
  lesson's ordering practice.
- **The subject is always said.** Persian drops it (رفتم، خسته‌ام، باران می‌آید); English never does
  (I went, I'm tired, *It*'s raining).
- **he / she.** او is both: Persian speakers say *he* for a woman and *she* for a man. Lesson 2, and back
  in every story.
- **a / an / the.** Persian has یک and ـی but no word for *the*, and را only for objects. Lessons 3 and 17
  (no *the* for things in general: *I like coffee*), back often.
- **Adjectives before the noun**, never plural (the ezafe pulls the other way: ماشینِ قرمز → *a red car*).
- **Plurals after numbers.** Persian keeps the noun singular (دو کتاب); English doesn't (*two books*).
- **do / does / did** in questions and negatives: Persian has nothing like them (just the voice or آیا, and
  نـ on the verb).
- **-s for he/she/it** in the present.
- **Present simple vs continuous.** می‌روم is both *I go* and *I'm going*.
- **The present perfect.** Persian says دو سال است که اینجا زندگی می‌کنم (present), English *I've lived
  here for two years*; and *ago*, not *before* (دو روز پیش → *two days ago*).
- **Verbs that look like adjectives in Persian**: موافقم → *I agree* (not *I am agree*); بیست سال دارم →
  *I'm twenty* (not *I have twenty years*).
- **Comparisons with "than"**, not *from* (بزرگ‌تر از → *bigger than*).
- **Relative clauses without the extra pronoun** (مردی که او را دیدم → *the man I saw*, not *the man that
  I saw him*).
- **One negative only** (هیچ‌کس نیامد → *Nobody came*, not *Nobody didn't come*).
- **Reported speech moves the tense back** (گفت خسته است → *He said he was tired*), and *if / when* clauses
  take the present, not *will* (اگر بیاید → *if he comes*).
- **want someone to** (می‌خواهم که بیایی → *I want you to come*, not *I want that you come*).
- **Countable and uncountable**: اطلاعات is plural in Persian, *information* never is.
- **Prepositions**: *married to* (not *with*), *afraid of* (not *from*), *depend on*, *angry at*.
- **Pronunciation notes** (tips): no vowel before *s* + consonant (*school*, not *eschool*; *street*);
  *w* is not *v*; *th*; short and long vowels (*ship / sheep*).
- **Politeness**: ta'ârof doesn't travel. Americans take a first "no, thanks" at its word, and say "yes,
  please" the first time.

## Order

1. Who you are and who others are: *be*, he/she, a/an/the, adjectives, possessives, questions with *be*
   (unit 1).
2. Things around you: *have*, plurals, *there is*, places, asking for things, directions (unit 2).
3. Every day: the present simple with *-s* and *do*, then the present continuous (unit 3).
4. Plans and abilities: *can*, *want to*, *going to*, *will*, *have to / should*, suggestions (unit 4).
5. The past (unit 5), then experiences, *for / since*, comparisons and quantities (unit 6).
6. Opinions, relative clauses, conditionals, reported speech (unit 7), and sounding natural: *make / do /
   take / get*, phrasal verbs, the passive, American politeness, tag questions (unit 8).

About 40% A1 (lessons 1–19), 40% A2 (20–38), 20% B1 (39–48).

## Units and lessons

### Unit 1 · اولین گفت‌وگوها (First conversations) (A1)
| # | id | capability | grammar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | say-who-you-are | Say hello and who you are | I'm / I am, my name is, I'm from; the verb comes second; the subject always said |
| 2 | talk-about-other-people | Talk about other people | he / she / it / they + is / are (او is two words in English) |
| 3 | name-things | Point at things and name them | a / an; this / that; it; the |
| 4 | describe-things | Describe people and things | adjective before the noun; adjectives never plural; be + adjective |
| 5 | say-whose-it-is | Say whose it is | my, your, his, her, our, their; Sara's; whose? |
| 6 | ask-yes-no-questions | Ask yes/no questions | Are you…? Is it…? (the verb moves first); isn't / aren't / I'm not; short answers |
| 7 | ask-where-what-who | Ask where, what, who and how | question words + be; How old are you? I'm 30 |
