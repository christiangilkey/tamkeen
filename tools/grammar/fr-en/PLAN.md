# French → English: the plan

American English, explained in natural French (written for a French speaker, not translated) to someone
who knows no grammar. 48 lessons in 8 units: 20 A1, 18 A2, 10 B1. Every lesson is a capability, opens
with the sentence the learner can say by the end, then meaning, form, mistakes, practice ending in "your
turn" (« À vous »).

## What is hard for a French speaker, and so gets the most time

- **One present in French, two in English.** *Je travaille* is *I work* (habit) or *I'm working* (now).
  Present simple (with its third-person **-s**) first, the continuous a little later, then both together.
- **do / does / did** in questions and negatives: French has nothing like it (*Tu parles anglais ?* →
  *Do you speak English?*; *As-tu un stylo ?* → *Do you have a pen?*). It comes back in every tense.
- **be where French says avoir**: *I'm 25*, *I'm hungry*, *I'm cold*, *you're right*, *I'm afraid*.
- **a/an with jobs** (*She's a doctor*), and **no article for things in general** (*I like cats*, *Life is
  short*): the reverse of French.
- **his / her by the owner**, not by the noun (*sa mère* → *his mother* or *her mother*); **it** for every
  thing (*la table* → *it*).
- **Adjectives before the noun, never plural** (*red cars*).
- **The present perfect vs the passé composé**: *I saw him yesterday*, not *I have seen him yesterday*;
  *I've lived here for three years* (not *I live here since…*, from *depuis*); *I've just arrived*
  (*je viens d'arriver*).
- **When + present** for the future (*when I arrive*, not *when I will arrive*, from *quand j'arriverai*).
- **make / do** (both *faire*); **I agree** (not *I am agree*); **want someone to** (not *I want that you
  come*); **to** for purpose (not *for to buy*).
- **Countable and uncountable nouns**: *some advice*, *information*, *furniture* (never plural).
- **Pronunciation notes** where they come up: *h* is said (*hi, happy, hungry*), *th* (*this, think*), the
  three sounds of *-ed* and of *-s*, *can / can't*.

## The spiral: where each structure comes back (`from`)

- **be**: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 9 (*there is*) → 17 (with *-ing*) → 23 (*was / were*) → 24 → 42 (passive).
- **a / an / the / no article**: 2 (jobs) → 5 → 6 (*the*) → 13 (*I like cats*) → 14 → 46.
- **present simple and -s**: 10 → 11 → 12 → 13 → 16 → 17 (vs continuous) → 35 (*when* + present).
- **do**: 11 → 12 → 15 → 23 (*did*) → 47 (*So do I*).
- **questions**: 4 → 11 → 15 → 23 → 31 (*Have you ever…?*) → 36 → 43 (*asked if*) → 47 (tags).
- **his / her / it**: 2 → 6 → 8 → 27.
- **present continuous**: 17 → 24 (past continuous) → 29 (arrangements) → 33 (*I've been waiting*).
- **can / could**: 5 (*Can I have…?*, a phrase) → 18 → 30 → 36.
- **going to / will**: 20 → 29 → 35 → 48 (*gonna*).
- **the past simple**: 21 → 22 → 23 → 24 → 25 → 32 (vs present perfect) → 43 → 45.
- **the present perfect**: 31 → 32 → 33 → 34 → 42 (*has been built*).
- **object pronouns**: 27 → 38 (*pick it up*) → 43 (*told me*).
- **conditionals and would**: 5 (*I'd like*) → 35 → 36 → 39 → 48 (*I'd*).
- **-ing**: 13 (*I like swimming*) → 17 → 44.

## Conventions

- American English: *apartment, vacation, movie, cell phone, check, store, subway, fall, fries*;
  *color, favorite, center*; *gotten*; *Can I get…?*; *have* rather than *have got*.
- The French explanations follow French typography, like the French basics course: a no-break space
  (U+00A0) before `? ! : ;` and inside « »; straight apostrophes. English examples have English
  punctuation.
- Words come mostly from the English basics course's 1000 words.
- No audio files: examples are read by the learner's card voice.
