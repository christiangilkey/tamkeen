# Korean → English: the plan

American English taught to a Korean speaker, with every explanation written in natural Korean (해요체,
the friendly style of a good teacher), comparing with Korean wherever that helps. No readings
(`"reading": ""`); pronunciation comes as short tips where a sound is hard for Korean speakers.

## What is hard for a Korean speaker, and the order that follows from it

- **The subject is always said.** Korean drops it whenever it's clear (배고파요, 추워요); English never
  does: *I'm hungry*, *It's cold*. Lesson 1 starts with it, and lesson 6 gives the empty *it* (weather,
  time, distance) a lesson of its own.
- **"Be" must be said with adjectives.** In Korean an adjective is a verb (피곤해요), so *I tired* is the
  classic mistake: lesson 3.
- **Word order is subject, verb, object**, with time and place at the end (*I study English at home every
  day*), the reverse of Korean: lessons 7 and 11, and back in every lesson that builds a sentence.
- **A/an, the, and plurals** don't exist in Korean: *a* with jobs (2), plurals (5), countable and
  uncountable (17), and *a* vs *the* (16), coming back throughout.
- **있어요 is two things in English**: *there is* (7) and *have* (8).
- **He and she** are rarely said in Korean speech (그/그녀 are written words), so learners mix them up
  (4, 12).
- **Third-person -s** (12) and **do** in questions and negatives (13, 14).
- **Answering negative questions**: Korean 네 agrees with the question (네, 안 먹어요); English *yes/no*
  follows the fact (*No, I don't*). A lesson of its own (14), and a mistake shown again later.
- **Tenses**: the progressive (20), the past (22–25), *will* vs *going to* (26–27: *I'll* is -(으)ㄹ게요),
  and the **present perfect**, which Korean doesn't have as a form (35–38; *for/since* is where Korean's
  present 10년 동안 살고 있어요 leads to *I live here for 10 years*).
- **Politeness without speech levels**: English has no 존댓말, so politeness comes from *Could you…?*,
  *please*, *I'd like* (21), and *you* is fine for anyone (no 당신 problem).
- **너무**: Korean uses it for *very* (너무 맛있어요); English *too* is negative (34).
- **Modifiers after the noun**: Korean puts the describing clause first (어제 본 영화); English after it,
  with *who/which/that* (40).
- **Pronunciation tips**: *f/p*, *v/b*, *l/r*, *th*, *z/j*, final consonants and the extra 으 vowel
  (*street*, not *seuteuriteu*).

## The spiral: where each big structure comes back

- **The subject and *it***: 1, 3, 6, then in every lesson's mistakes when Korean would drop it (12, 20,
  22, 39).
- ***Be***: am/are/is (1–3), *there is* (7), was/were (22), *be going to* (26), the progressive (20, 25),
  the passive (46).
- ***Do***: questions (13), negatives and short answers (14), the past *did* (24), *Do you want to…?* (15).
- **Articles and plurals**: *a* with jobs (2), plurals (5), *a/the* (16), countable (17), *the best*
  (32), generic plurals again in likes (15) and comparisons (31).
- **Tenses on a line**: present simple (11–14) → continuous (20) → past (22–25) → future (26–28) →
  present perfect (35–38) → *used to* (41) → reported speech moves back a tense (45).
- **Yes/no after negative questions**: 14, then again in 24 (*Didn't you…?*) and 35 (*Haven't you…?*).
- **Prepositions**: place (9, 10), time (18), *for/since* (36), phrasal verbs (42).
- **Politeness**: can (19), could/would (21), *I'll* offers (27), suggestions (28), advice (29).
- **Connecting ideas**: *and/but* (11), *because/so* (30), *when* (25, 39), *if* (39, 44), *who/that* (40).
- **Modifiers before vs after the noun**: adjective before the noun (5, 33), relative clauses after it
  (40), the passive participle (46).
