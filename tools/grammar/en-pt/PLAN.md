# English → Brazilian Portuguese: the plan

Brazilian Portuguese as Brazilians speak it in everyday life, with **você** for "you" (and *a gente*
beside *nós* for "we"). The examples are the standard spoken language of São Paulo, Rio or Brasília on
TV: correct spelling and accents (the 2009 spelling: *ideia*, *voo*, *tranquilo*), never European forms
(*estou a falar*, *tu*, *autocarro*, *pequeno-almoço*). Where speech and careful writing differ in ways a
learner meets every day, the lesson says so and shows both: *pra* / *para*, *tem* / *há*, *me liga* /
*ligue-me*, *vi ele* / *eu o vi*, *fala* / *fale* in commands.

**Why this order.** An English speaker finds Portuguese words easy and its endings hard. So the course
gives a few useful forms first as chunks (*sou, estou, quero, queria, tem*), then the system behind
them once the learner has used them. Three things make Portuguese what it is and come back all the way
through: **two verbs "to be"** (*ser* for who and what, *estar* for how and where), **gender and the
little words that join** (*o/a*, *do/da*, *no/na*), and **verb endings that say who and when**
(so *eu* is usually dropped). Brazilian habits a textbook often skips are taught early because
learners hear them at once: answering with the verb instead of *sim*, *tem* for "there is", *a gente*,
pronouns before the verb, *estar* + -ndo.

About 40% A1 (lessons 1–19), 40% A2 (20–37), 20% B1 (38–48).

## Units and lessons

### Unit 1 — First conversations (A1)
| # | id | capability | grammar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `say-who-you-are` | Say hello and who you are | *ser*: *eu sou*, *você é*; dropping *eu*; *de* + city; *do/da/dos* + country (as chunks); questions by tone |
| 2 | `say-how-you-are` | Say how you are | *estar*: *estou*, *está*; adjectives *-o/-a* by speaker; *obrigado/obrigada*; *ser* vs *estar* (who vs how right now) |
| 3 | `say-yes-and-no` | Answer yes and no like a Brazilian | *não* before the verb; answering with the verb (*Fala? Falo.*); *não… não*; *eu* and *você* forms of a few verbs |
| 4 | `ask-for-what-you-want` | Order in a café | *quero* / *queria* (polite); *um/uma*; nouns have gender; *quero* + verb |
| 5 | `name-things` | Point at things and name them | *o/a/os/as*; plural *-s*; *isso*, *esse/essa*, *aquele*; *O que é isso?* |
| 6 | `ask-questions` | Ask for information | *o que, onde, quando, quem, como, qual, quanto, por que*; question word first or last (*Você mora onde?*); *Como se diz…?* |

## The spiral: where the big structures come back

- **ser / estar**: who you are (1) → how you are (2) → where things are, with *ficar* (7) → describing
  (10: *é bonito* vs *está bonito*) → what's happening (18) → the past (*era / estava*, 28–29) →
  how something made you feel (*ficar*, 36) → results (*está fechado*, 46).
- **Questions**: by tone (1) → yes/no answers with the verb (3) → question words (6) → *Você gosta de
  quê?* (14) → *Você já…?* (27) → *será que…?* (42) → follow-up questions (48).
- **Articles and the little words that join**: *um/uma* (4) → *o/a* (5) → *no/na* (7) → *ao/pra* (12) →
  *do/da* (14) → *a casa da Ana* (34).
- **The present**: a few forms (3) → the whole regular system (13) → irregulars (16) → reflexives (17) →
  *há* + present for "for" (30).
- **ir**: going (12) → plans (19) → *vamos* (22) → *fui* (26) → *ia* (28) → *ia* in reported speech (43).
- **querer**: *quero/queria* (4) → polite *queria* (37) → *quero que* + subjunctive (38).
- **The past**: perfeito (25, 26) → *já/ainda não* (27) → imperfeito (28) → both together (29) → *há*
  for "ago" (30) → reported (43) → *tinha feito* (44).
- **Pronouns**: reflexive (17) → *me passa* in commands (23) → object pronouns (24) → *seu* vs *dele* (34).
- **Subjunctive**: the polite command form (23, not named yet) → *quero que* (38) → advice and doubt (39)
  → *quando/se* + future subjunctive (40) → *se eu tivesse* (41) → *embora* (47).

From lesson 4 on, every lesson has at least one practice question marked `from` an earlier lesson.
