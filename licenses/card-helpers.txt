Example sentences and the free Mnemonic maker: Tatoeba, Wiktionary, WordNet, FrequencyWords, CMUdict (optional download)

With no AI key, "Add words" can fill in a real example sentence for a word, and the review card's Mnemonic
maker finds English words that sound like it. What they use is not part of this program: the app downloads
it the first time it is needed (docs/lexicon/<language>-examples.json.gz and en-keywords.json.gz, made by
tools/build_examples.py and tools/enrich_examples.mjs).

Example sentences and their English translations:

- Tatoeba (https://tatoeba.org), a collection of sentences and translations written by its contributors.
  The sentences are available under the Creative Commons Attribution 2.0 France license
  (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/fr/); some are in the public domain (CC0). Each sentence
  kept here keeps its Tatoeba number, so it can be found at https://tatoeba.org/en/sentences/show/<number>
  with its author. Sentences were chosen (short ones, a few for each word) and nothing in them was changed.
- The English Wiktionary's usage examples (https://en.wiktionary.org, by the Wiktionary contributors), as
  extracted by Wiktextract (Tatu Ylonen, https://kaikki.org), under the Creative Commons
  Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International License (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).
  The same files keep each word's pronunciation (IPA) from Wiktionary, and Chinese words' pinyin from
  CC-CEDICT (CC BY-SA 4.0, https://cc-cedict.org). The files are shared under CC BY-SA 4.0.

- Bisaya example sentences from John U. Wolff, A Dictionary of Cebuano Visayan (1972), public domain in the USA, as
  transcribed for Project Gutenberg (ebook 40074, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/40074) by Jeroen Hellingman, Scott
  Robertson and the Online Distributed Proofreading Team: each example with its English, in today's spelling.
- Arabic dialect sentences also from the app's own dialect courses (docs/course/ar-<dialect>), and the dialects' own
  pronunciations from the English Wiktionary's Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf, Hijazi, Moroccan and Tunisian entries.
- Each language's Basics course sentences (docs/course/<language>/course.json: a short everyday sentence with its
  translation for each of the 1,000 commonest words), Tamkeen's own, added by tools/enrich_examples.mjs. That step also
  notes which dictionary word each word of the sentences is a form of (plurals, verb forms, Arabic words with a
  pronoun ending), worked out from the language's meanings file (the English Wiktionary, CC BY-SA 4.0).
