Tagalog roots and verb forms (optional downloads)

The Tagalog word popup shows a word's root (kumain: kain), its affix (-um-), its verb focus (actor, object,
directional, benefactive, locative ...) and its aspect forms (complete, progressive, contemplative), and the
root finder (web/js/lexicon/tagalog.js) knows the roots of about 10,000 verb forms. They come from:

1. The English Wiktionary (https://en.wiktionary.org), written by the Wiktionary contributors and extracted
   by Wiktextract (Tatu Ylonen, https://github.com/tatuylonen/wiktextract; data from https://kaikki.org):
   each Tagalog entry's etymology ("From kain + -um-"), the trigger in its headword line ("1st actor
   trigger") and its conjugation table, and the derived terms listed under a root. Wiktionary's text is
   available under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International License
   (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) and the GNU Free Documentation License; see
   wiktionary.txt.

2. UD_Tagalog-NewsCrawl (https://github.com/UniversalDependencies/UD_Tagalog-NewsCrawl), a Universal
   Dependencies treebank of Tagalog news text from the Leipzig Tagalog Corpus, annotated by Angelina Aquino,
   corrected by hand following guidelines by Elsie Marie Or, Maria Bardaji Farre and Nikolaus Himmelmann,
   and checked by Lester James Miranda, with many student annotators; made possible by the Deutsche
   Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG). Licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0
   International License (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Only its word-level
   annotations are used (each word form, its lemma, which is the root, its part of speech and its
   Voice, Aspect and Mood features), for the roots of verb forms and for which focus and aspect a verb form
   is; none of its sentences are kept.

The files made from them (docs/lexicon/tl-meanings.json.gz and tl-roots.json.gz, by tools/build_meanings.py
--grammar tl) are shared under the same CC BY-SA 4.0 license.
