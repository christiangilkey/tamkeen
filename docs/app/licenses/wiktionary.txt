Word meanings for "No AI": Wiktionary, through Wiktextract (optional download)

People who choose "No AI" in Settings -> Word definitions can download a dictionary of word
meanings for the language they are learning. It is not part of this program; the app downloads it
only when the learner asks (docs/lexicon/<language>-meanings.json.gz, made by tools/build_meanings.py).

The meanings come from the English Wiktionary (https://en.wiktionary.org), written by the
Wiktionary contributors, as extracted by Wiktextract (Tatu Ylonen, https://github.com/tatuylonen/wiktextract;
data from https://kaikki.org). Tatu Ylonen: Wiktextract: Wiktionary as Machine-Readable Structured
Data, Proceedings of the 13th Conference on Language Resources and Evaluation (LREC), pp. 1317-1325,
Marseille, 20-25 June 2022.

Wiktionary's text is available under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International
License (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) and the GNU Free Documentation License.
The dictionary files made from it are shared under the same CC BY-SA 4.0 license. They keep each
word's part of speech, its definitions (shortened to the first few, each at most 180 characters),
how it is written in full, its reading or root, and which word a form belongs to; everything else
(examples, etymologies, translations, pronunciations) is left out, except that the Bisaya (Cebuano) file
also takes the Bisaya words given in English entries' translation tables (the English word and the
sense it is a translation of) for words that have no Bisaya entry of their own. The English file keeps only
common words and phrases made of them; Chinese spellings are linked to their simplified forms using
CC-CEDICT's character table (CC BY-SA 4.0). The Hindi file also keeps the forms in each word's inflection
table and its romanization (written the way the app writes readings), and gives Hindi's postpositions of more
than one word (के लिए "for") to their main word; see hindi-sources.txt.

The German word table and common-word list also take a few facts from the German entries: which spellings
the 1996 reform replaced, which are Switzerland's, and how often each word is written with a capital in the
entries' example sentences (see german-sources.txt). The German meanings file keys ß as ss, so today's
spelling, the old one and Switzerland's find the same entries.

The Persian word table (docs/lexicon/fa-lemmas.json.gz, made by tools/build_persian.py, also an optional
download) is made from the same Wiktionary data: the Persian headwords, the forms in their conjugation and
declension tables (which verb, noun or adjective each form belongs to) and each verb's present and past stem,
with wordfreq's word counts (CC BY-SA 4.0) choosing which forms to keep. It is shared under CC BY-SA 4.0 too.
