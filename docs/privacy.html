Privacy Policy

Tamkeen · Last updated October 9, 2026

Tamkeen is an open-source app that runs on your own devices. Your library, transcripts and vocabulary stay on your devices and in your own Google Drive. The only data that ever reaches a service run for the app is what you choose to use with the optional friends & sharing features. This page explains where your data goes when you use each feature.

Data stored on your device

Your podcast subscriptions, episode lists, downloaded audio, transcripts, saved vocabulary, settings and any API keys you enter are stored only on your device, in the app's data folder. Transcription runs entirely on your device. Audio is never uploaded for transcription.

Anki companion (optional)

If you install the app's add-on in Anki on the same computer, it tells the app four numbers a day: how many cards you reviewed, how many you failed, how many became mature, and how many are still due. They go from Anki to the app on your own computer and are kept with your game progress. For this, the contents of your cards are never read or sent.

If you also switch on "Keep my saved words and my Anki deck in step" in Settings, the app and Anki exchange the cards of one deck (called "Tamkeen" unless you rename it): their text, their tags, and how each card is doing in your reviews. On a computer this goes between Anki and the app on that same computer, through the add-on. On a phone it goes between AnkiDroid and the app on that phone, through the permission Android asks you for. Your other decks are never read or changed, nothing is sent to the developer or to any server, and it stays off until you switch it on. Words that arrive from Anki become part of your saved vocabulary, so they are included in Google Drive sync if you use it.

Podcasts

The app downloads podcast RSS feeds and audio directly from the podcast's own servers, as any podcast player does. Those servers see a normal download request from your device.

Web pages and the in-app browser

When you add a web page, the app downloads it directly from that website, as a browser would, and keeps its text on your device (and in your own Google Drive if you use sync). The in-app web browser loads the sites you visit directly; those sites see a normal visit from your device and may set their own cookies, which stay on your device. The developer receives nothing about the pages you read or import. A site shown in the browser has no access to your library, vocabulary or keys. The browser's start page is part of the app: a list of links to news sites in the language you are studying and a Google search box. It loads the small icon of each listed site from Google's icon service, and a search you type there goes to Google, as in any browser. Sites you keep on the start page with the star are a list of addresses and page names kept on your device (and in your own Google Drive if you use sync); their icons are loaded the same way.

Trending podcasts

The "Trending" list comes from Apple's public podcast charts. When you open that list, your device asks Apple for the charts of a few countries where the language you are studying is spoken, and for the feed addresses of the podcasts in them. Apple sees a normal request from your device (including its internet address); it is not told who you are or what is in your library, and no Apple account is involved.

YouTube videos

YouTube videos are never downloaded. They play inside the app in YouTube's own embedded player, which uses YouTube API Services: watching one is like watching it on any website, so YouTube (Google) may set cookies, show ads and count the view, under the Google Privacy Policy and the YouTube Terms of Service. "Open YouTube" shows YouTube's own website in the app's browser, exactly as described under "Web pages and the in-app browser" above (if you sign in to YouTube there, that sign-in stays on your device). When you tap a video, your device also asks YouTube for that video's captions; their text is kept on your device (and in your own Google Drive if you use sync) so the words can be clicked. When you follow a channel, your device asks YouTube for that channel's name and picture; the list of channels you follow is kept the same way, on your device and in your own Google Drive. The developer receives nothing about the videos you add or watch, or the channels you follow.

Instagram reels (optional)

"Get 10 reels" works on the computer only. The app opens Instagram's website in a window of its own, where you sign in to Instagram yourself (directly, or through Facebook if that is how you sign in); that sign-in stays on your computer, in the app's browser storage, and is never read by the app or sent anywhere else. Instagram (Meta) sees a signed-in visit from your computer: searches for popular videos in the language you are studying, and visits to the pages of creators you chose. "Get random reels" (on the computer, and on the phone when cloud transcription is on) searches Instagram, as you, for a few everyday words in that language picked at random. If you choose "From my Instagram saves", the app also reads, from your own Saved page, the names of your saved collections and which posts are in the one you pick; those stay on your computer. To write down what is said in a reel, the app fetches that reel's video once, listens to it on your own computer, and deletes it straight away. All that is kept is the reel's address, its creator's username and the words spoken (on your device, and in your own Google Drive if you use sync). On the phone a reel plays from Instagram's own embed page inside the app, so Instagram sees those views and may set cookies, under the Meta Privacy Policy. A few saved reels (mostly ones with a song from Instagram's music library) are only played by Instagram to people who are signed in: for those the app asks you to sign in to Instagram on the phone, in the app's own browser, and then plays the reel's ordinary Instagram page. That sign-in stays on your phone, in the app's browser storage, and is never read by the app; Instagram then sees those views as yours. The developer receives nothing about the reels you get or watch. This feature is not provided or endorsed by Instagram.

Google sign-in and Google Drive sync (optional)

If you choose to sign in with Google, the app requests only these permissions:

  • drive.appdata: access to a private, hidden app folder in your own Google Drive. The app cannot see, read or change any of your other Drive files.
  • openid and email: to show which Google account is signed in.

The app uses this folder to sync your subscriptions, transcripts, vocabulary (with your own notes and your answers in the vocab reviewer), cached word definitions and, if you enable it, compressed copies of transcribed audio between your own devices. This data goes directly between your devices and Google. The developer never has access to it. Google's handling of data in your Drive is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

You can stop syncing at any time with Sign out in the app's settings, and remove the app's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. You can delete the app's Drive data from Google Drive's settings (Manage apps → Tamkeen → Delete hidden app data).

Tamkeen's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Friends, sharing and quizzes (optional)

These features only work if you choose Connect with Google under "Friends & sharing" in the app's settings. They use an online database hosted by Supabase on behalf of the app. When you connect, it stores:

  • your Google account's email address and a user ID (to sign you in), and the username you choose. Other users can look up a username to send a friend request; they never see your email address;
  • your friend requests and friends;
  • messages between you and a friend: text, voice notes (private audio files) and the words or folders you send them, each with a short audio clip of its sentence when it has one. Only the two of you can read them, and they are temporary: a text message is deleted once the other person has seen it and left the chat, a voice note once it has been played, shared words once they are added or dismissed, and anything unopened after 30 days. You can also unsend a message. The paper-airplane button in a player sends a friend what you are watching, reading or listening to: its title, kind and address, and for a YouTube video or an Instagram reel the words your app has for it (its captions or transcript), so it is ready to study on their device. Your friend sees only the title and kind until they press Accept, and the message is deleted when they accept or dismiss it. Your own audio and video files cannot be sent;
  • your "What I'm studying" card and note (up to three items, for example a podcast episode title, or a YouTube, Instagram reel, web page, Spotify, Apple Music or IMDb link with its title and picture), visible only to your friends. Titles and pictures come from those sites' public link-preview services (for a web page, from the page itself), and playing a song loads Spotify's or Apple's own player. When you tap an item on a friend's card it opens in your own app the same way as if you had added it yourself: your device fetches the video's captions, the page, the reel or the podcast, and it joins your library;
  • guilds you create or join: the guild's name and tag (visible to any signed-in user, next to members' usernames) and who its members are (visible to the guild's members and to each member's friends). People in the same guild can see each other's "What I'm studying" card and message each other, like friends;
  • a profile photo, if you add one. It is stored as a small picture at a public web address, so anyone who has that address can see it. You can remove it at any time;
  • whether you are online: while the app is open it tells the service so about once a minute. Only your friends and guild-mates can see it;
  • the words in shared folders, visible only to that folder's members;
  • for the creature game, if you open its Friends page or battle a friend: your team of up to three creatures (their generated looks, names, items and chosen moves), how accurately you answer questions about your own words (one percentage), your skill rating and league, and this week's totals (days you reached your daily goal, and right answers). Also, for the small bar next to your name in friends' lists: the language you are learning, how much XP you have earned today, and the look and name of the one creature you chose to show off. Friends and guild-mates can see these. The results of battles between you and a friend, and gifts of game items or eggs between you, are stored so the other person can see or open them; battle results older than 60 days and opened gifts older than 30 days are cleared as you keep using the game Your words themselves, their meanings and the questions you are asked are never sent.
  • invitations to live quizzes. During a live quiz, the questions, answers and scores are passed between the players' devices through Supabase and are not stored.

Your podcasts, transcripts, private vocabulary, notes and API keys are never sent to this service. Quiz questions are written by the AI provider of the player who starts the quiz, using their own API key (see below). You can disconnect any time in settings. To delete your account and everything stored for it, open an issue at the address below and it will be removed.

AI word definitions and quizzes (optional)

If you add your own API key for an AI provider (Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, OpenAI or xAI Grok) and tap a word, the app sends that word, the sentence it appears in, the neighbouring sentences and the podcast and episode titles to the provider you chose, using your key. When you start a vocabulary quiz, the words in the chosen folder and their saved meanings are sent the same way so the provider can write the answer choices. That provider's privacy policy applies to those requests. No other data is sent, and nothing is sent unless you have added a key.

Cloud transcription (optional)

Transcripts are normally written on your own computer and no audio leaves it. If you turn on "Cloud transcription" in Settings and add your own API key for a speech-to-text provider (Groq or OpenAI), the audio of what you transcribe is sent to the provider you chose, using your key, and the provider sends back the words: from the computer, only the parts of the audio that contain speech; from the phone, the episode's audio file (or, for a large file that is not an MP3, its web address, which Groq then fetches itself), your own video or audio file if it is small enough to send, and the video of each Instagram reel you choose. That includes the audio of files you added yourself, so leave this off for recordings you want to keep private. Nothing else about you or your library is sent. That provider's privacy policy applies to those requests. Your key is stored only on that device and is never part of a data export. On the phone, choosing reels from your own Instagram saves reads, from Instagram's website and as you, the names of your saved collections and the posts in the one you pick (see "Instagram reels" above); those stay on your phone. The developer receives none of this.

Analytics and advertising

The app contains no analytics, tracking, advertising or crash-reporting services.

Updates

When you click "Check for updates", the app asks GitHub for the latest release version.

Children

The app does not knowingly collect any personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes and contact

Changes to this policy will be posted on this page. For questions, open an issue at github.com/christiangilkey/tamkeen/issues.
