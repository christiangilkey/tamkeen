Privacy Policy

Tamkeen · Last updated November 10, 2026

Tamkeen is provided by Caliber Products LLC, a Florida limited liability company ("we", "us"), which is responsible for the personal data described here. Contact: tamkeen.app.help@gmail.com.

Tamkeen is an open-source app that runs on your own devices. Your library, transcripts and vocabulary stay on your devices and, if you turn on sync, in your own Google Drive. Two services run for Tamkeen can receive data about you: the online database behind Friends, chat and guilds, only if you turn those on (see Friends); and, in the web app only, a go-between server that fetches podcasts, web pages, YouTube captions and Instagram reels for your browser (see The web app). This page explains where your data goes for each feature.

Data stored on your device

Your podcast subscriptions, episode lists, downloaded audio, transcripts, saved vocabulary, settings and any API keys you enter are stored on your device, in the app's data folder (and in your own Google Drive if you use sync; see below). Transcription normally runs on your own computer, and the audio stays there. If you turn on Cloud transcription, the audio is sent to the provider you choose (see "Cloud transcription").

Pronunciation practice

On a review card, the "Say it" button uses the microphone to listen to you saying the word, then plays the word's own audio followed by your attempt so you can compare them. Your attempt is held in the app's memory only for the few seconds this takes and is then discarded. It is never saved to a file, never sent anywhere and never leaves your device. The microphone is on only while the button shows "Listening".

Your own recordings on flashcards (optional)

When you add words with "Add words" on the Vocab page and choose to add your own audio, the microphone is on only while the card shows "Recording…", or you choose a sound file yourself. The recording is kept with that card, like a card voice: on your devices, and in your own Google Drive if you use sync. It is sent nowhere else. Words you ask Tamkeen to fill in (meanings, example sentences) are looked up in the built-in dictionary on your device, or sent to your own AI provider with your own key, as when you look up a word.

Scanning words from a photo (optional)

When you choose "Scan" in "Add words" and take, choose, drop or paste a photo of a list of words, the photo is read in one of two ways. If you use AI and your provider can read pictures, a smaller copy of the photo is sent to that provider (Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, OpenAI or xAI Grok) with your own key, only to read the words on it, and the words come back; that provider's privacy policy applies. Otherwise the photo is read on your device itself (with Tesseract, whose program and language data are downloaded once from this website when you first use it), and it is sent nowhere. Either way the photo is held in the app's memory only while it is read and shown beside the words found; it is never saved, kept or synced, and it is gone when you close "Add words". On Android, the copy the phone's camera app makes when you take the photo is deleted once it has been read. Tamkeen uses the camera and your photos only when you choose to.

Anki companion (optional)

If you install the app's add-on in Anki on the same computer, it tells the app four numbers a day: how many cards you reviewed, how many you failed, how many became mature, and how many are still due. They go from Anki to the app on your own computer and are kept with your game progress. For this, the contents of your cards are never read or sent.

If you also switch on "Keep my saved words and my Anki deck in step" in Settings, the app and Anki exchange the cards of one deck (called "Tamkeen" unless you rename it): their text, their tags, and how each card is doing in your reviews. On a computer this goes between Anki and the app on that same computer, through the add-on. On a phone it goes between AnkiDroid and the app on that phone, through the permission Android asks you for. Your other decks are never read or changed, nothing is sent to the developer or to any server, and it stays off until you switch it on. Words that arrive from Anki become part of your saved vocabulary, so they are included in Google Drive sync if you use it.

Podcasts

The app downloads podcast RSS feeds and audio directly from the podcast's own servers, as any podcast player does. Those servers see a normal download request from your device.

Web pages and the in-app browser

When you add a web page, the app downloads it directly from that website, as a browser would, and keeps its text on your device (and in your own Google Drive if you use sync). The in-app web browser loads the sites you visit directly; those sites see a normal visit from your device and may set their own cookies, which stay on your device. Nothing about the pages you read or import is stored by us (in the web app, see "The web app and its go-between server" below). A site shown in the browser has no access to your library, vocabulary or keys. The browser's start page is part of the app: a list of links to news sites in the language you are studying and a Google search box. It loads the small icon of each listed site from Google's icon service, and a search you type there goes to Google, as in any browser. Sites you keep on the start page with the star are a list of addresses and page names kept on your device (and in your own Google Drive if you use sync); their icons are loaded the same way.

The web app and its go-between server

The web app is served by GitHub Pages; GitHub sees your device's internet address, as for any website (see GitHub's privacy statement). Web browsers don't let one website read another, so the web app asks Tamkeen's go-between server, which runs for us on Cloudflare Workers, to fetch things for it: podcast feeds and audio, web pages you import, YouTube captions and video details, and Instagram reels' public pages. That server receives the address of each item and your internet address, passes on no cookies, and stores nothing. Cloudflare, which runs it, may keep basic technical logs for a short time under its own privacy policy. The computer and phone apps fetch these things directly and don't use it.

Trending podcasts

The "Trending" list comes from Apple's public podcast charts. When you open that list, your device asks Apple for the charts of a few countries where the language you are studying is spoken, and for the feed addresses of the podcasts in them. Apple sees a normal request from your device (including its internet address); it is not told who you are or what is in your library, and no Apple account is involved.

YouTube videos

YouTube videos are never downloaded. They play inside the app in YouTube's own embedded player, which uses YouTube API Services: watching one is like watching it on any website, so YouTube (Google) may set cookies, show ads and count the view, under the Google Privacy Policy and the YouTube Terms of Service. "Open YouTube" shows YouTube's own website in the app's browser, exactly as described under "Web pages and the in-app browser" above (if you sign in to YouTube there, that sign-in stays on your device). When you tap a video, your device also asks YouTube for that video's captions; their text is kept on your device (and in your own Google Drive if you use sync) so the words can be clicked. When you follow a channel, your device asks YouTube for that channel's name and picture; the list of channels you follow is kept the same way, on your device and in your own Google Drive. Nothing about the videos you add or watch, or the channels you follow, is stored by us (in the web app, see "The web app and its go-between server").

Netflix and the "Tamkeen for Netflix" browser extension (optional)

Netflix titles play only in Netflix's own player, on Netflix's own website, signed in with your own Netflix account; Tamkeen never sees your Netflix password. In a web browser on a computer, the optional "Tamkeen for Netflix" extension adds Tamkeen's subtitle bar to Netflix's tab. It notices the subtitle file Netflix's player downloads for the language you are learning, downloads that file once more from Netflix's servers, and shows its lines as words you can click. The extension keeps, in your browser only: the settings the Tamkeen web app gives it (the languages, how words are explained and, if you use AI, your own AI key, which is sent only to the AI provider you chose, exactly as in the app), downloaded dictionaries, the answers already looked up, and the words you save and the titles you watch until the Tamkeen web app takes them into your library (from where they sync to your own Google Drive if you use sync). Netflix's subtitles stay in your own library and are never sent to friends or anyone else. The extension sends nothing to the developer and has no analytics. The extension's own privacy policy gives the details.

Instagram reels (optional)

"Get 10 reels" works on the computer only. The app opens Instagram's website in a window of its own, where you sign in to Instagram yourself (directly, or through Facebook if that is how you sign in); that sign-in stays on your computer, in the app's browser storage, and is never read by the app or sent anywhere else. Instagram (Meta) sees a signed-in visit from your computer: searches for popular videos in the language you are studying, and visits to the pages of creators you chose. "Get random reels" (on the computer, and on the phone when cloud transcription is on) searches Instagram, as you, for a few everyday words in that language picked at random. If you choose "From my Instagram saves", the app also reads, from your own Saved page, the names of your saved collections and which posts are in the one you pick. The list of posts stays on the device that read it; the collection names and which one you picked are kept on your device, and in your own Google Drive if you use sync (so your phone and computer show the same list). To write down what is said in a reel, the app fetches that reel's video once, listens to it on your own computer, and deletes it straight away. All that is kept is the reel's address, its creator's username and the words spoken (on your device, and in your own Google Drive if you use sync). On the phone a reel plays from Instagram's own embed page inside the app, so Instagram sees those views and may set cookies, under the Meta Privacy Policy. A few saved reels (mostly ones with a song from Instagram's music library) are only played by Instagram to people who are signed in: for those the app asks you to sign in to Instagram on the phone, in the app's own browser, and then plays the reel's ordinary Instagram page. That sign-in stays on your phone, in the app's browser storage, and is never read by the app; Instagram then sees those views as yours. Nothing about the reels you get or watch is stored by us (in the web app, see "The web app and its go-between server"). This feature is not provided or endorsed by Instagram.

Google sign-in and Google Drive sync (optional)

If you choose to sign in with Google, the app asks for these permissions:

  • drive.appdata: access to a private, hidden app folder in your own Google Drive. The app cannot see, read or change any of your other Drive files.
  • openid and email: to show which Google account is signed in.
  • only if you use "Today's review as a to-do", and only the first time you send it: tasks (Google Tasks) or calendar.events (Google Calendar). Tamkeen uses them only to add or update one task or event a day (its title, "Tamkeen review", the day, the time you choose and the estimated minutes) and to check whether that task or event still exists. It never reads, changes or deletes your other tasks or events.

The app uses this folder to sync your subscriptions, transcripts, vocabulary (with your own notes and your answers in the vocab reviewer), cached word definitions, the API keys you add for AI providers (so a key added on one of your devices works on the others) and, if you enable it, compressed copies of transcribed audio between your own devices. This data goes directly between your devices and Google. The developer never has access to it. Google's handling of data in your Drive is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

You can stop syncing at any time with Sign out in the app's settings, and remove the app's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. You can delete the app's Drive data from Google Drive's settings (Manage apps → Tamkeen → Delete hidden app data).

Tamkeen's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Friends, sharing and quizzes (optional)

These features only work if you choose Connect with Google under "Friends & sharing" in the app's settings. They use an online database hosted by Supabase on behalf of the app. When you connect, it stores:

  • your Google account's email address, Google's ID for your account and the times you signed in (to sign you in), and the username you choose. Other signed-in users can find you only by typing your exact username (to send a friend request); they can't list everyone's profiles, and they never see your email address;
  • your friend requests and friends;
  • messages between you and a friend (only friends can message each other): text, voice notes (private audio files) and the words or folders you send them (with their meanings, your notes on them and their sentences), each with a short audio clip of its sentence when it has one. Only the two of you can open them in the app. They are stored on Supabase's servers and are not end-to-end encrypted, so we, as the database's administrator, could technically see them; we look at stored content only to handle a report, a legal request or a technical fault. They are temporary: a text message is deleted once the other person has seen it and left the chat, a voice note once it has been played, shared words once they are added or dismissed, and anything unopened after 30 days (the service clears old messages every night). You can also unsend a message. The paper-airplane button sends a friend what you are watching, reading or listening to: its title, kind and address, and the words your app has for it (captions or transcript, or the text of a web page or book), so it is ready to study on their device. For one of your own recordings, only its title and transcript are sent, never the file itself. Your friend sees only the title and kind until they press Accept, and the message is deleted when they accept or dismiss it;
  • your "What I'm studying" card and note (up to three items, for example a podcast episode title, or a YouTube, Instagram reel, web page, Spotify, Apple Music or IMDb link with its title and picture), visible only to your friends. Titles and pictures come from those sites' public link-preview services (for a web page, from the page itself), and playing a song loads Spotify's or Apple's own player. When you tap an item on a friend's card it opens in your own app the same way as if you had added it yourself: your device fetches the video's captions, the page, the reel or the podcast, and it joins your library;
  • guilds you create or join: the guild's name and tag (visible to any signed-in user, next to members' usernames) and who its members are (visible to the guild's members and to each member's friends). People in the same guild can see each other's "What I'm studying" card, online status and game team; only those who are also friends can message each other;
  • a profile photo, if you add one. It is stored as a small picture at a public web address, so anyone who has that address can see it. You can remove it at any time;
  • whether you are online: while the app is open it tells the service so about once a minute. Only your friends and guild-mates can see it;
  • the words in shared folders, visible only to that folder's members;
  • for the creature game, if you open its Friends page or battle a friend: your team of up to three creatures (their generated looks, names, items and chosen moves), how accurately you answer questions about your own words (one percentage), your skill rating and league, and this week's totals (days you reached your daily goal, and right answers). Also, for the small bar next to your name in friends' lists: the language you are learning, how much XP you have earned today, your flashcard streak, and the look and name of the one creature you chose to show off. And from fishing: the fish you keep in your aquarium (each one's kind, rarity and length, up to six), your biggest catch of the week, and how many Fishdex entries you have. Friends and guild-mates can see these. The results of battles between you and a friend, and gifts of game items or eggs between you, are stored so the other person can see or open them; battle results older than 60 days and opened gifts older than 30 days are cleared every night. Your words themselves, their meanings and the questions you are asked are never sent.
  • invitations to live quizzes. During a live quiz, the questions, answers and scores are passed between the players' devices through Supabase and are not stored;
  • the people you block (only you can see this list). A blocked person can't message you, send you friend requests, see your card, online status or game team, or challenge you;
  • reports: if you report someone, your reason and note, and a copy of the reported message and of their username, photo address and card at the time, because messages disappear. If someone reports you, the same about you. Only we can read reports;
  • to stop abuse, a count of the friend requests, messages and reports each account sent in the last day (kept for two days).

Your podcasts, transcripts, private vocabulary and notes are sent to this service only when you choose to send something to a friend, and then only that item. Your API keys never are. Quiz questions are written by the AI provider of the player who starts the quiz, using their own API key (see below). Supabase keeps short technical logs, including internet addresses, for security and troubleshooting, under its own policy.

You can disconnect any time in settings. To delete your online account and everything stored with it, use Settings → Account & sync → Friends & sharing → Delete my online account (it happens straight away), or email tamkeen.app.help@gmail.com from the email address of your Google account (we delete it within 7 days). Guilds and shared folders you founded pass to their longest-standing member; words you added to someone else's shared folder stay there without your name; reports you sent stay, without your name. You can block anyone and report a person or a message: see the Terms of Service (section 6).

AI features (optional, with your own key)

Nothing is sent to an AI provider unless you add your own key and use one of the features below. Each request goes from your device to the provider you chose (Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, OpenAI or xAI Grok), using your key and paid by your account with them; that provider's terms and privacy policy apply.

  • Looking up a word: the word, its sentence and the sentences around it, and the title of the episode, video, page or show.
  • Add words: the words you typed, scanned or picked, so meanings and example sentences can be filled in; for a scan, a smaller copy of the photo.
  • Mnemonic maker: the word, its meaning and reading, your languages, and keywords you already turned down.
  • Grammar lessons, "Check with AI": the exercise, its model answers and the sentence you wrote. Please don't write private information there.
  • Live quizzes: the words in the chosen folder and their meanings (with the key of the player who starts it).
  • Captions written by Gemini: for a YouTube video with no captions, if you ask, the video's link and the language you study are sent to Google Gemini with your Gemini key; Gemini watches the public video and sends back its words.

Providers may keep requests for a time under their own policies. With a free (unpaid) Google Gemini key, Google may use what is sent to improve its products, and people may read it. AI answers can be wrong; they are marked "AI" in the app. The providers have their own minimum ages (some require 18); please meet them before adding a key.

Cloud transcription (optional)

Transcripts are normally written on your own computer and no audio leaves it. If you turn on "Cloud transcription" in Settings and add your own API key for a speech-to-text provider (Groq or OpenAI), the audio of what you transcribe is sent to the provider you chose, using your key, and the provider sends back the words: from the computer, only the parts of the audio that contain speech; from the phone, the episode's audio file (or, for a large file that is not an MP3, its web address, which Groq then fetches itself), your own video or audio file if it is small enough to send, and the video of each Instagram reel you choose. That includes the audio of files you added yourself, so leave this off for recordings you want to keep private. Nothing else about you or your library is sent. That provider's privacy policy applies to those requests. Your key is stored on that device (and in your own Google Drive if you use sync, so your other devices can use it) and is never part of a data export. On the phone, choosing reels from your own Instagram saves reads, from Instagram's website and as you, the names of your saved collections and the posts in the one you pick (see "Instagram reels" above); the posts stay on your phone, and the collection names and your pick are shared with your computer through your own Google Drive if you use sync. The developer receives none of this.

Card voices (optional)

Flashcards without a recording of their own are read out by the voice you choose in Settings → Card voices. "This device's voice" uses the speech built into your phone, computer or browser; nothing is sent by the app. "My computer" makes the recording on your own computer with a voice model downloaded there; nothing is sent. If you choose Azure and add your own Microsoft Azure Speech key, the word and its sentence (and nothing else about you or your library) are sent from your device straight to Microsoft's speech service in the region you chose, using your key, and Microsoft sends back the spoken audio; Microsoft's privacy policy applies to those requests. A recording made by Azure or by your computer is kept with the card on your devices and in your own Google Drive if you use sync. Your Azure key is stored like your other keys: on the device, and in your own Google Drive if you use sync, and never in a data export.

Analytics and advertising

The app contains no analytics, tracking, advertising or crash-reporting services.

Updates and downloads

When you click "Check for updates", the app asks GitHub for the latest version. When you ask for them, the app downloads speech-recognition models and computer voices from Hugging Face, Piper's program from GitHub, NVIDIA's graphics libraries from PyPI, and dictionaries, courses, example sentences and the photo reader from Tamkeen's website on GitHub Pages; books and audiobooks come from LibriVox, Project Gutenberg, Wikisource or Aozora Bunko. These are ordinary downloads: those sites see your device's internet address, and nothing about you or your library is sent.

Your rights, why we use data, and how long we keep it

Your rights. Wherever you live, you can ask us to see, correct, export or delete the personal data held on our service by emailing tamkeen.app.help@gmail.com; we answer within 30 days. If you are in the EU or UK, you can also object to or restrict how we use it, and complain to your local data protection authority. Data on your own devices and in your own Google Drive is already in your hands.

Why we use it (legal bases, EU/UK). We use the data on our service to provide the online features you turn on (performance of our agreement with you), short technical logs to keep the service secure (our legitimate interest in security), and anything the law requires us to keep (legal obligation). We don't sell or share personal data, we don't use it for advertising, and we don't build profiles of you or make automated decisions about you.

Where it is stored. Supabase, Cloudflare and GitHub are US companies, so data on our service may be processed in the United States, under those companies' data protection terms and standard contractual clauses.

How long we keep it.

  • Profile, username, photo, friends, guild memberships and "What I'm studying": until you change them or delete your account.
  • Text messages: until seen and the chat is closed, at most 30 days. Voice notes: until played, at most 30 days. Shared words and sent items: until accepted or dismissed, at most 30 days.
  • Online status: only the last time you were seen, replaced about every minute.
  • Battle results: 60 days. Opened gifts: 30 days. Live quiz questions and answers: not stored.
  • Blocks: until you unblock the person or delete your account. Reports: until we have handled them, then 90 days; at most a year. The daily counts of what was sent: two days.
  • Server logs: kept by Supabase and Cloudflare for a short time under their own policies.

Do Not Track. Tamkeen doesn't track you across other websites, so it works the same whether or not your browser sends a Do Not Track signal. Other companies' services that you open inside Tamkeen (such as YouTube or Instagram) follow their own policies.

Children

The online features (Friends, chat, guilds, usernames and sharing), which are the only place Tamkeen's own service stores personal data, are for people 16 or older everywhere. Before you can use them, the app asks your month and year of birth once. It keeps only whether you may use them (or the month you turn 16), on your own devices and in your own Google Drive, never your date of birth, and none of it is sent to us. Under 16, everything else in the app works as usual and nothing about you is stored on our service. We do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 16; if we learn that we have, we delete that account and its online data. A parent or guardian who believes this has happened can email tamkeen.app.help@gmail.com, and anyone can report an account in the app (⋯ → Report → "They seem to be under 16").
